Most website chatbots fall into one of two traps. Either they only know a handful of canned answers, or they are a general AI that will answer anything, confidently, whether or not it is true about your business. Neither is something you'd want talking to your customers.

There is a third kind: a chatbot that answers **only from your own documents**, shows where each answer came from, and hands the conversation to a person when your documents don't cover it. The technical name is RAG, short for retrieval-augmented generation. This guide explains what that means without the jargon.

## The example: a moving company's help desk

The example is a sample build I made for **Harbor & Pine Moving Co., a fictional moving company**. Its help-desk bot answers customers from two documents, the pricing guide and the FAQ, in the website chat or by email. Damage claims always go to a person.

A customer asks: *"Do you move pianos, and what does it add?"*

The bot answers: *"Yes: uprights and baby grands, crated in-house if needed. A coordinator prices it."* And it names its source: **FAQ, Specialty items**.

That last part matters most. Anyone can check the answer against the document it came from.

## How it works in general, in two halves

A bot like this is built the same way whatever the business. Harbor & Pine's uses the website chat and email, and its two documents; yours might use other channels and sources.

### Getting your documents ready

This half runs once, and again whenever a document changes.

1. **Your documents** go in: help articles, service pages, PDFs, and price lists in Google Sheets. This is the only material the bot is allowed to answer from. Nothing outside it counts.
2. Long documents are **split into passages** of a few paragraphs each, so a search can return just the part that matters.
3. Each passage is turned into a kind of fingerprint of its meaning, so passages about similar things sit close together. These are stored in a database (Supabase), ready to search.

### Answering a question

This half runs every time someone asks something.

1. The question comes in from the website chat, Telegram or email.
2. The question gets the same kind of fingerprint, and the database finds the **five passages** closest in meaning.
3. Claude, the AI model, gets the question and those five passages, with one rule: **answer only from what is in them.**
4. Then a check: did the passages actually hold the answer?
   - **Yes:** the answer goes back where the question came from, with the passage it came from.
   - **No:** the bot doesn't guess. It **hands the conversation to a person**, with the whole chat so far, so the customer never has to repeat themselves.

A bot can also be set up to offer open times for a call when a visitor is ready to buy, instead of sending them to a form.

## Why this beats a general chatbot

- **It can't make up your prices.** If the price isn't in your price sheet, the bot says so and passes it on.
- **Every answer is checkable.** The source travels with the answer.
- **It knows when to stop.** Some questions, like damage claims for this mover, should always reach a person. You decide which.
- **It stays current.** Update a document and the bot answers from the new version.

## What you'd need

To build one for your business, I'd need:

1. **The documents** customers' questions are really about: your FAQ, price list, policies, service pages. Most businesses already have these, scattered across a few places.
2. **Where questions arrive:** your website chat, email, Telegram, or more than one.
3. **What always goes to a person:** refunds, complaints, anything you'd rather handle yourself.
4. **Who takes over** when the bot hands off, and how they'd like to be told.

A good first version answers the questions your team is tired of answering, and passes everything else on cleanly.
