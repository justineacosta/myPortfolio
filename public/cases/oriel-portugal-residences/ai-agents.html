AT A GLANCE

Two patterns, one rule

Every build below is one of two patterns. An architect approves anything Claude drafts for a buyer’s inbox; the chat answers only from the sheets.

  • 1

    RAG chatbots

    The Q&A bot searches Oriel’s residence sheets and notes first, then answers with the sheet it used.

  • 2

    AI agents

    They take one viewing, gather what they need from HubSpot, Airtable and the sheets, and finish the job.

  • 3

    The architect’s yes

    The project architect approves anything Claude drafts for a buyer’s inbox. The chat answers only from the sheets.

HOW THE Q&A BOT WORKS

Search the sheets first, then answer

HOW AN AGENT WORKS

A viewing starts it. Claude plans, uses one tool at a time and checks the result until the job is done. Click any step.

GUARDRAILS

Four rules every build follows

They are what make a bot safe to put in front of someone choosing a home.

  • G1

    Answers only from Oriel’s documents

    The bot answers from the passages it found and names the sheet or note. No source, no answer: the chat goes to the studio.

  • G2

    Never quotes a price or a date not in the residence sheet

    Oriel publishes no prices, so the bot never gives one. A date the sheet does not give goes to the studio too.

  • G3

    Claude’s drafts for a buyer wait for a yes

    Every follow-up an agent drafts stops for the architect in Telegram before it goes out. The bot never emails; in the chat, the sheets are the limit.

  • G4

    Every run is logged

    Each question, tool call and send lands in a run log, and on the buyer’s HubSpot contact when the visitor is already a contact.

PLAIN ENGLISH

Every term on this page, in one line

Plain English for the technical words above.

RAG
Retrieval-augmented generation: search Oriel’s own documents first, then answer from what was found.
Residence sheet
One document per residence: its rooms, sizes, aspect, sun hours, materials and status.
Revision note
The plain account of what changed when a residence’s model gets a new revision.
Passage
A room or a change cut from a longer document, as the search returns it and the answer cites it.
Embedding
A list of numbers that captures what a piece of text means, so similar ideas sit close together.
Vector search
Finding the stored passages whose embeddings sit closest to the question’s.
pgvector
A Postgres add-on that stores embeddings and runs vector search, here inside Supabase.
Hand-off
Passing the chat to the studio, with everything said so far.
Agent
An AI that takes a goal, picks its own tools and works step by step until the job is done.
Trigger
The event that starts a run: the evening before a viewing, or the architect’s notes after it.
Tool call
One action an agent asks for, like reading a buyer’s HubSpot history or a residence sheet.
Loop-back
A cable back to an earlier step: the agent tries again, or redoes a draft the architect sent back.
Approval gate
A point where the run stops until the project architect says yes.
Claude
Anthropic’s AI model. It plans, reads and writes in every build here.
n8n
The workflow tool that runs each build: it listens for triggers and makes the tool calls.
Supabase
A hosted Postgres database, used here as the store the Q&A bot searches.
HubSpot
The CRM where Oriel keeps every buyer, their viewings and what they have been told.
Airtable
Where Oriel keeps its projects, its residence sheets and the revisions table OR-03 watches.
