01

Before anything is built

The outcome, the entry points and every way a patient’s story ends.

Why plan first

Range makes three promises a flow has to keep: Mara will be in touch before your visit, a superbill comes after each visit, and six plan visits are used within four months. One rule sits above them all: nothing clinical in any flow. This page pins all of it down before n8n, Make or Zapier is opened.

The clinical rule

Clinical content never enters an automation, Claude, HubSpot or Airtable. The flows carry scheduling and billing data only. Clinical notes, and the codes on a superbill, stay with Mara and her notes system.

Outcome

Every evaluation booked gets its intake email and Mara knows of it, every visit’s superbill waits in Gmail with its billing lines filled for Mara to add the codes, and every six-visit plan opens when paid and reminds its holder twice, with nothing clinical in any flow.

Entry points

Eight entry points: an Initial evaluation booked in Cal.com, a visit paid at checkout on Stripe, a visit row ready in Airtable, the plan paid on Stripe, a schedule each morning, RP-01 or RP-H02 calling RP-H01, n8n’s own Error Trigger and a webhook that Make and Zapier report failures to.

Every way a job ends

  • Mara told
  • Superbill drafted
  • Plan open
  • Reminded

Out of scope

  • Clinical notes, the written plan of care and anything a patient tells Mara: they stay in her notes system, which no flow reads or writes
  • The procedure and diagnosis codes on a superbill, which Mara adds by hand
  • Follow-up and free 15-minute call bookings, which get Cal.com’s own confirmation
  • A cancellation inside 24 hours, the Clinic Q&A bot and the inbox, planned on the AI agents page
  • Refunds and changes to a plan, which Mara makes by hand in Stripe

Naming convention

One vocabulary across n8n, Make and Zapier.

Why it matters

Three platforms, one clinician. When a run fails between two visits, its name has to say which flow it was, wherever it ran.

Rule of thumb

If a run log does not read like a sentence, rename the steps.

Workflows

RP-[number] · [Title]
RP-01 · Evaluation booking
RP-H02 · Log a failed run

The same codes on every platform. The number is the build order; helpers get an H.

Scenarios and Zaps

RP-[number] · [Title] · [part]
RP-02 · Superbill per visit · draft
RP-03 · Six-visit plan · expiring

A flow a platform has to split keeps one code, and the last word says which part.

Steps

[Verb] [object]
Keep scheduling only
Draft the superbill
Open the plan

Default names like “HTTP Request1” or “Router 2” never survive a build.

Webhook paths

/rp/[source]
/rp/failed

One path per source, all lowercase.

Connections

RP · Gmail (Make)

Each platform holds its own connection, and the name says which one to renew.

Payment Links

The flows filter on each link’s ID; the name is what Mara sees in Stripe. Test and live links never share a name.

03

Where the data lives

Three Airtable tables, scheduling and billing only, and two sheet tabs.

Source of truth

Airtable holds what the flows need: patients, visits and plans. Cal.com holds the calendar, Stripe the payments, and Google Sheets only keeps logs. Mara’s notes system holds everything clinical, and no flow touches it.

Scheduling and billing only

No table has a field for a reason for visit, a symptom, a diagnosis, a code or a note, and none is added later. The handover lists adding one as a break.

Patients

Name Email Phone Date of birth Billing address Evaluation at Visits Plans

One row per patient, matched on email. Date of birth comes from the evaluation booking and Billing address from their first Stripe checkout, both for superbills.

Visits

Patient Type Date of service Paid by Plan Fee Amount paid Stripe payment Payer name Payer email Ready Ready changed Superbill Drafted at

One row per visit. Type is Initial evaluation or Follow-up; Paid by is Card or Plan; Superbill is empty or Drafted. Fee is a formula: $215, $185, or $170 for a plan visit, which is also its Amount paid. Ready is a formula: patient, type, date and paid-by all filled. Ready changed is a Last modified time field that watches those four.

Plans

Buyer Buyer name Buyer email Bought on Expires Visits used Visits left Status Stripe payment Two-left sent Expiry sent

One row per plan. Expires is Bought on plus four months; Visits used counts its plan visits in Visits, and Visits left is six less that. Status is a formula: Active, Used up or Expired. The Two visits left and Expires in three weeks views feed the morning Zaps.

RP-01n8n

Evaluation booking

7 nodes, 0 decisions.

Watch for

  • The clinical rule: clinical content never enters an automation, Claude, HubSpot or Airtable. This flow carries scheduling and billing data only, and clinical notes stay in Mara’s own notes system, which no flow reads or writes.
  • Cal.com’s Initial evaluation page asks for a name, email, phone and date of birth, the last for superbills. Those are its only text questions: Additional notes is hidden and no other question is added. Cal.com has no date field, so date of birth is a short text answer, used only as a date of birth.
  • Keep scheduling only has Include Other Input Fields off, so anything else Cal.com sends stops there. RP-01’s settings save no executions, successful, failed or manual, so n8n keeps no copy of a booking, even of a run that fails.
  • Cal.com’s trigger fires for every event type. The Filter keeps the Initial evaluation and lets a follow-up or a free 15-minute call go quietly; those get Cal.com’s own confirmation.
  • The intake email is a fixed template, so it sends on its own. It asks no health questions: Mara takes the history in person, at the start of the visit. Cal.com’s own confirmation for the evaluation is turned off, or replaced by it, so the patient reads one email.
  • Save the patient is a Create or update matched on email, so a second booking from the same email updates the same row. There is no search, so nothing can come back empty and end the run.
  • If a step fails, RP-H02 tells Mara which step failed. The run itself is not saved, so she finds the booking in her Cal.com calendar and sends the intake email herself.

Outcome

Every new evaluation is met with the intake and what-to-wear email within a minute, and Mara knows who is coming and when, so she can be in touch before the visit, as the site promises.

Trigger

n8n’s Cal.com Trigger hears every new booking in Mara’s calendar; a Filter keeps the Initial evaluation.

Exits

  • Intake sent, Mara told
  • Not an evaluation
RP-02Make

Superbill per visit

12 nodes, 1 decision.

Watch for

  • The clinical rule: clinical content never enters an automation, Claude, HubSpot or Airtable. This flow carries scheduling and billing data only, and clinical notes stay in Mara’s own notes system, which no flow reads or writes.
  • No procedure code, diagnosis code, symptom or treatment note enters RP-02, Airtable or Stripe. Visits has no field for them, the Superbill template leaves two blank lines for them, and Make never opens a draft again once it is made.
  • Mara adds the codes in the superbill itself: she reads them in her notes system, types them into the Gmail draft and sends it. No tool reads her notes system and no flow reads the sent superbill, so the codes reach the patient by her hand alone.
  • The superbill goes out when Mara sends it, not when Make drafts it. Her Gmail Drafts folder is the queue of superbills waiting for codes.
  • Make gives a scenario one trigger, so RP-02 is two scenarios in one folder: RP-02 · Superbill per visit · paid logs a Stripe payment as a visit, and RP-02 · Superbill per visit · draft watches Visits for any row that is ready, paid by card or from a plan. The Automation page draws them as one flow.
  • Payment is taken at checkout, after the visit. Mara opens the visit’s Stripe Payment Link for the patient, by hand, Initial evaluation at $215 or Follow-up visit at $185, and they pay on Stripe’s page. No flow sends the link. The Six-visit plan link belongs to RP-03, and the filter after the trigger lets it go.
  • Find the patient is Airtable’s Make an API call, not Search Records, so it always returns one bundle with a list, and the router reads the list’s length. A search that found no one would end the route and lose the payment.
  • Stripe can send an event twice. Log the visit is an upsert merged on the Stripe payment, so a repeat updates the same row, and a visit already Drafted is never drafted again.
  • A plan visit has no payment. Mara logs it on the plan in Visits, and Fee is a formula: $215, $185, or $170 for a plan visit.
  • Every module that writes has an error route that posts the step and the error, never the visit, to /rp/failed, and ends in Break, so Make keeps the run to retry.

Outcome

Every visit, paid by card or from a plan, has its superbill waiting in Range’s Gmail drafts within 15 minutes, every billing line filled in, so Mara only adds the codes from her notes and sends it.

Trigger

Make’s Stripe module hears a visit paid at checkout. Airtable’s Watch Records, every 15 minutes, starts the second scenario for any visit row that is ready.

Exits

  • Drafted, Mara adds the codes
  • No match, with Mara
RP-03Zapier

Six-visit plan

16 nodes, 0 decisions.

Watch for

  • The clinical rule: clinical content never enters an automation, Claude, HubSpot or Airtable. This flow carries scheduling and billing data only, and clinical notes stay in Mara’s own notes system, which no flow reads or writes.
  • Plans holds a buyer, dates, a count and two reminder dates, and no clinical field. The reminders say how many visits are left and until when, never whether more visits would help: that is a clinical question, for Mara on a call or at a visit.
  • The patient buys the plan themselves, at the published $1,020, on Stripe’s own page. No flow refunds, extends or ends a plan early: any such change is Mara’s, made by hand in Stripe and on the plan’s row.
  • A Zap has one trigger, so RP-03 is three Zaps: RP-03 · Six-visit plan · bought, · two left and · expiring. The Automation page draws the two morning Zaps one after the other, as they run, two left at 7 and expiring at 7:15, with the connector between them marked A separate Zap: neither waits for or depends on the other. A plan can get both on the same morning; each says something different.
  • Visits left counts the plan visits Mara logs in Visits. A visit she has not logged yet is not counted, so a reminder can come late, never early.
  • Expires is a formula, four months from Bought on, so the welcome, the reminders and the views always show the same date.
  • The plan is shareable within a household. Mara logs a household member’s visit on the buyer’s plan, and its superbill names the person she saw.
  • The welcome says every plan starts with an Initial evaluation, as the site does, so a plan bought first still begins there.
  • Find Many Records stops the Zap when it finds nothing, so a morning with no plan due sends nothing.
  • A failed step is caught by a Zapier Manager zap that posts it to /rp/failed.

Outcome

Every plan opens the moment it is paid, with its expiry date in writing, and its holder is reminded when two visits remain and again three weeks before the four months are up.

Trigger

Stripe’s Checkout Session Completed starts the first Zap; Schedule by Zapier starts the two reminder Zaps every morning, at 7 and 7:15 Mountain time.

Exits

  • Plan open, Mara told
  • Reminded
  • Nothing due

Helpers

Two small workflows the others call.

Why helpers

Alerts and failures are written once, in n8n, and all three platforms report failures to them. Make and Zapier message Mara with their own Telegram modules.

RP-H01 · Alert Mara

Every message n8n sends Mara reads the same way and lands in her Telegram chat.

RP-H02 · Log a failed run

Every failed run, on any of the three platforms, is logged and reaches Mara with a link to it.

Build order

Five phases, in the order they are built.

Order

Foundation first, then one platform at a time, in the order a patient meets them: the booking, the first paid visit, then a plan.

  1. 1 · Foundation

    • Create the Airtable tables, Patients, Visits and Plans, with scheduling and billing fields only, and the Two visits left and Expires in three weeks views
    • Add the connections on each platform: RP · Airtable, RP · Gmail, RP · Telegram, RP · Cal.com, RP · Stripe (test)
    • Create the Sheets tabs: Failed runs, Test matrix
    • Build RP-H01 in n8n, then RP-H02 with RP-01 (not RP-H01) as the covered error workflow

  2. 2 · Bookings on n8n

    • Set up the Initial evaluation event type in Cal.com, 60 minutes: name, email, phone and date of birth, Additional notes hidden, and its own confirmation turned off, or replaced by the intake email
    • Build RP-01 with Save failed, Save successful and Save manual executions all set to Do not save
    • Point Schedule your evaluation and Reserve at the Initial evaluation page
    • Run tests t01–t06

  3. 3 · Superbills on Make

    • Create the Initial evaluation ($215) and Follow-up visit ($185) Payment Links in Stripe test mode, taking the billing address, and show Mara how to open the right one for a patient at the end of a visit
    • Write the Superbill template with Mara: Range’s address, her credentials, NPI and tax ID, and two blank code lines
    • Build both RP-02 scenarios, paid and draft, in one Make folder, every writing module with an error route to /rp/failed
    • Run tests t07–t13 with test patients and Stripe test cards

  4. 4 · Plans on Zapier

    • Create the Six-visit plan Payment Link ($1,020) in test mode and point Start a plan at it
    • Build the three RP-03 Zaps: bought, two left and expiring
    • Add the Zapier Manager zap that posts failures to /rp/failed
    • Run tests t14–t22

  5. 5 · Test and hand over

    • Switch to RP · Stripe (live) and the live Payment Links
    • Check the clinical rule with Mara: every table, template, view and log holds scheduling and billing data only
    • Run the whole matrix again with Mara
    • Walk Mara through the handover page, and turn every flow on
08

Handover

What Range keeps.

Written for Mara

One page, no jargon, for the person who sees every patient. It lives next to the flows.

What it does

  • Sends every new evaluation the intake and what-to-wear email within a minute, and tells Mara who is coming and when.
  • Logs every paid visit and drafts its superbill in Gmail, every billing line filled, for Mara to add the codes and send.
  • Asks Mara to match a payment from an email on no patient’s row.
  • Drafts a plan visit’s superbill at $170 once Mara logs the visit on the plan.
  • Opens every six-visit plan the moment it is paid, and sends the welcome with its expiry date.
  • Reminds a plan’s buyer when two visits remain, and three weeks before the plan expires.

What breaks it

  • Renaming a field in Patients, Visits or Plans, or changing the Type, Paid by or Superbill choices.
  • Adding a clinical field to any table, or another question to a Cal.com booking page: either would let clinical content into a flow.
  • Changing a price in Stripe without changing the Fee formula and the templates.
  • Replacing a Payment Link without updating the filters in RP-02 and RP-03.
  • Editing the Two visits left or Expires in three weeks views.
  • A connection expiring on one platform but not the others.

Who to call

Justine Acosta, who built it. Failures already reach Mara on Telegram with a link to the run.

Where the logs are

Every failed run, from any of the three platforms, is a row in the Failed runs tab and a Telegram message, holding the step and the error and never a patient’s data. Every visit keeps whether its superbill was drafted, and every plan its visits left and the reminders sent.
