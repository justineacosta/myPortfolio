From Request a bid to a closeout the superintendent signed

Every trigger, flow, bot and agent behind Ironline’s site, wired as one graph: a bid emailed to bids@, logged and assigned by the bid desk’s rota, summarised for the superintendent who answers it within one business day; a storm call answered by people; a crew member’s question answered from the field SOPs; a near-miss turned into a company bulletin within 48 hours; and a closeout package sent to the owner within ten business days of energization, once its superintendent approves.

Ironline is a safety trade, so no flow, bot or agent gives, drafts or changes a switching, clearance, outage or energized-work instruction, and none overrides the job brief: those come from the job brief, the superintendent and system operations, and every task starts with a documented job brief. Storm and emergency calls go to the 24/7 line, (614) 555-0199, answered by people, and no flow dispatches a crew. The safety director approves anything Claude drafts for company-wide distribution, within 48 hours of the report being filed. In IL-01, IL-02 and IL-03 Claude drafts only the near-miss bulletin, as the Near-miss bulletin agent; the Bid triage agent runs beside IL-01, not in it, and writes for one superintendent only. The bot answers crews live, without a yes, from the SOPs only and always with the source. The superintendent approves every closeout package before it reaches the owner, and nothing Claude writes reaches an owner’s inbox. The acknowledgement, the reminders and the closeout email are fixed templates and send on their own. No flow reaches a utility’s system operations: outages and switching are done by people.

PLAIN ENGLISH

Every term on the map, in one line.

Plain English for the technical words above.

n8n
The automation tool bid requests, the Bid triage agent, the bot and the failed-run log run in.
Make
The automation tool near-miss reviews run in, with the Near-miss bulletin agent as its draft step.
Zapier
The automation tool closeouts run in, with Human in the Loop and Paths.
Claude
Anthropic’s AI model. It answers in the bot, summarises a bid for one superintendent, and drafts the near-miss bulletin.
Airtable
Where Ironline keeps its bid requests, the Bid duty rota, its near-miss reports, its projects and closeout packages.
Gmail
The bids@, safety@ and closeout@ mailboxes, which send every fixed template.
Telegram
The chat app where superintendents hear of bids, crews ask the bot, the safety director hears of drafts, and a team hears of a failed run.
Supabase
The database that stores the SOP passages and equipment rows the bot searches.
Google Sheets
Where the equipment sheet and the Failed runs tab are kept.
Template
A fixed message with blanks for the name, the project or the day. No AI writes it.
Rota
The Bid duty table, where the bid desk puts one superintendent on bid duty each week.
Superintendent
The person who reviews a bid and writes the response, runs the job, and approves its closeout. Never a salesperson.
Safety director
The person who approves every near-miss bulletin before it goes company-wide.
Project lead
The person who sets the Energization date and submits the closeout package.
Job brief
The documented brief every task starts with. Switching, clearance and energized-work steps come from it.
Switching
Taking a line out of service or putting it back, executed with system operations by people. No flow, bot or agent gives a step of it.
Near-miss
Something that nearly went wrong in the field, filed on the report form with no names and no blame.
Touches procedure
The flag Claude sets when a near-miss touches switching, clearance, an outage or energized work, so the safety director writes the lesson.
Human in the Loop
Zapier’s approval step: the superintendent gets an email link to a review page, and only their choice there counts.
Failed runs
The Google Sheets tab that logs each failed or halted run from n8n, Make or Zapier.
Approval gate
A hard stop. The safety director approves anything Claude drafts for company-wide distribution, within 48 hours of the report being filed. The superintendent approves every closeout package.
Agent
An AI with one job that reads what it needs, one call at a time, until the job is done.
