Every bid request, near-miss and closeout, planned before a single step is placed, and not one step that tells a crew how to switch.

Three flows and two helpers for Ironline Utility Infrastructure, the employee-owned, IBEW-signatory contractor at 2400 Corrugated Way, Columbus, with 340 people building transmission, distribution, substation and underground work across seven states since 1978. Bid requests to bids@ironlineutility.com run on n8n, near-miss reviews on Make and closeouts on Zapier. A bid goes to the superintendent on bid duty that week: the bid desk sets the Bid duty rota each week, so a person has already chosen who reviews that week’s bids, and the flow only applies their choice. Airtable holds the bid requests, the rota, the team, the near-miss reports, the projects and the closeout packages, Telegram carries every alert, and Google Sheets holds the logs. The site promises that a superintendent, not a salesperson, responds within one business day, that every near-miss is reviewed company-wide within 48 hours, and that closeout is delivered within ten business days of energization; each flow keeps one of those promises. No step, and no Claude step, gives, drafts or changes a switching, clearance, outage or energized-work instruction, and no flow dispatches a crew: storm and emergency calls go to (614) 555-0199, answered 24/7 by people.

01

Before anything is built

The outcome, the entry points and every way a request, a report or a closeout ends.

Why plan first

Ironline is a safety trade. Its crews work energized lines, and their switching and clearance steps come from a documented job brief, a superintendent and system operations, never from a message a flow sent. This page pins down, before n8n, Make or Zapier is opened, that no step crosses that line, and that every promise on the site has a step that keeps it.

The safety rule

The safety rule: no step in these flows, and no Claude step, gives, drafts or changes a switching, clearance, outage or energized-work instruction. Those come from the job brief, the superintendent and system operations, and every task starts with a documented job brief. Storm and emergency calls go to the 24/7 line, (614) 555-0199, answered by people. No flow reads a message for an emergency and no flow dispatches a crew: crews roll within six hours of a call, by a person’s decision.

Who says yes

The safety director approves every near-miss bulletin before it goes company-wide, and the project’s superintendent approves every closeout package before it reaches the owner. The bid acknowledgement, the reminders and the closeout email are fixed templates and send on their own. In these flows Claude drafts only the near-miss bulletin, which stays inside Ironline, and nothing Claude writes reaches an owner’s inbox. A bid goes to the superintendent on bid duty that week: the bid desk sets the Bid duty rota each week, so a person has already assigned the week’s superintendent, and anyone on the bid desk can reassign a request.

Outcome

Every bid request is logged, assigned to a superintendent and acknowledged within minutes, every near-miss reaches the company as an approved bulletin within 48 hours, and every energized project’s closeout reaches the owner within ten business days, approved by its superintendent.

Entry points

Ten entry points: an email to bids@, a business-morning check, a near-miss report filed, a twice-daily reminder, a bulletin approved, an energization date set, a closeout package submitted, IL-01 or IL-H02 calling IL-H01, n8n’s own Error Trigger and a webhook that Make and Zapier report failures to.

Every way a job ends

  • Assigned and acknowledged
  • Reviewed company-wide
  • Closeout sent, or sent back to the lead

Out of scope

  • Switching, clearance, outage and energized-work instructions: they come from the job brief, the superintendent and system operations, and no flow or Claude step gives, drafts or changes one
  • Storm and emergency response: the 24/7 line, (614) 555-0199, answered by people; no flow dispatches a crew, and crews roll within six hours of a call by a person’s decision
  • Outage requests and anything filed with a utility’s system operations, which people do by hand; no flow reaches a utility’s systems
  • Pricing a bid, and the response itself, which the superintendent writes
  • Changing a procedure after a near-miss: the safety director’s call, made through the job brief and system operations
  • The Team knowledge bot and the two agents, Bid triage and Near-miss bulletin, planned on the AI agents page
02

Naming convention

One vocabulary across n8n, Make and Zapier.

Why it matters

Three platforms, three teams, and alerts that arrive at 6 a.m. When a run fails, its name has to say which flow and which part it was, wherever it ran.

Rule of thumb

If a run log does not read like a sentence, rename the steps.

Workflows

IL-[number] · [Title]
IL-01 · Bid request
IL-H02 · Log a failed run

The same codes on every platform. The number is the build order; helpers get an H.

Parts

IL-[number] · [Title] · [part]
IL-01 · Bid request · one email
IL-02 · Near-miss review · send
IL-03 · Closeout · clock

A flow one workflow, scenario or Zap cannot hold is split into parts in one folder. The Automation page draws them as one flow.

Steps

[Verb] [object]
Find the thread
Draft the bulletin
Send the package

Default names like “HTTP Request1” or “Router 2” never survive a build.

Webhook paths

/il/[source]
/il/failed

One path per source, all lowercase.

Connections

IL · [Service] ([platform])
IL · Gmail (n8n)
IL · Anthropic (Make)
IL · Airtable (Zapier)

Each platform holds its own connection, and the name says which one to renew. Gmail is signed in as bids@ on n8n, safety@ on Make and closeout@ on Zapier.

Choice fields

Plain words, one fixed list each
New, Assigned, Responded, Not a bid
Filed, Awaiting approval, Approved, Sent
Awaiting, Approved, Sent back

Status on Bid requests, Status on Near-miss reports, and Superintendent review on Closeout packages, in that order. The flows, the templates and this page use the same words.

Airtable tables

Title case, one thing per table
Bid requests
Near-miss reports
Closeout packages

Flows read fields by name, never by position.

Airtable holds the bid requests and the rota, the team, the near-miss reports, the projects and their closeout packages. The drawings stay in bids@. Google Sheets holds the logs.

One vocabulary per field

Every choice field takes one fixed list, and the flows, the templates and this page use the same words for it.

No fault, no names

Near-miss reports has no field for who was at fault. Filed by is optional and read by the safety director alone; no step and no bulletin uses it.

IL-01n8n

Bid request

18 nodes, 3 decisions.

Watch for

  • The safety rule: no step in these flows, and no Claude step, gives, drafts or changes a switching, clearance, outage or energized-work instruction. Those come from the job brief, the superintendent and system operations, and every task starts with a documented job brief. Storm and emergency calls go to the 24/7 line, (614) 555-0199, answered by people. No flow reads a message for an emergency and no flow dispatches a crew: crews roll within six hours of a call, by a person’s decision.
  • In IL-01 that means the acknowledgement, a fixed template, ends with the 24/7 line. Nothing in IL-01 reads an email for urgency, so a storm or emergency sent to bids@ is logged and assigned like any request, and the sender is told to call (614) 555-0199, where people answer around the clock. No step promises a crew or a time.
  • A superintendent, not a salesperson, reviews every inquiry and responds within one business day, as the site says. The superintendent is whoever is on bid duty that week in the Bid duty rota. The bid desk sets the rota each Friday for the week ahead, so a person has already assigned the week’s superintendent; the flow only applies that choice to each request. It never reads the email to choose, and anyone on the bid desk can reassign a request on its row.
  • n8n hands a poll’s emails on together, and a search’s Always Output Data speaks for the whole batch, not for each email. So IL-01 is two parts: IL-01 · Bid request · inbox holds the two triggers and hands each email on its own, with Execute Sub-workflow set to Run once for each item, to IL-01 · Bid request · one email. Every search there sees one email, so an empty result means that email. The Automation page draws the two parts as one flow.
  • Find the thread has Always Output Data on, so an email on a thread with no request hands an empty item to New request?, which logs it as new. Only a new thread is logged, assigned and acknowledged, so a reply, or an auto-reply, never starts a second bid or a loop of acknowledgements.
  • Find who’s on duty has Always Output Data on, so a week with no one on the rota hands an empty item to On the rota?, which sends it to IL-H01: the bid desk group is told at once, with a link to the row, and assigns a superintendent by hand. The acknowledgement still goes.
  • Respond by is an Airtable formula: the end of the next business day after the email arrived, Monday to Friday, Eastern, skipping the holidays the bid desk lists in it. The superintendent writes the response themselves, from the thread in bids@, and sets Status to Responded. Each business morning at 6 a.m. Eastern, Find bids due today has Always Output Data on: every request still New or Assigned on its Respond by day reaches the bid desk group, and an empty result goes to Nothing due.
  • The acknowledgement is a reply on the sender’s thread from bids@, a fixed template with Append n8n Attribution off. It names no price, no date and no crew. Claude writes none of IL-01.
  • The trigger’s search skips mail from ironlineutility.com, so the acknowledgement and the superintendent’s own response from bids@ never start a run. The drawings stay in bids@: the row keeps a link to the thread, and the superintendents have delegate access to the inbox.
  • Log the request, Notify superintendent and Acknowledge the email retry three times. If one still fails, the run fails and IL-H02 alerts the bid desk with the step and a link to the run. Both parts of IL-01 keep their failed executions, so a person reads the email there, or in bids@, and finishes by hand.

Outcome

Every bid request to bids@ is logged and in a superintendent’s hands within minutes, and the sender hears at once that a superintendent will respond within one business day.

Trigger

n8n’s Gmail Trigger checks bids@ironlineutility.com every minute for mail from outside Ironline; a Schedule Trigger runs the due-today check at 6 a.m. each business morning.

Exits

  • Assigned by the rota, acknowledged
  • No one on the rota, bid desk told, acknowledged
  • Follow-up passed on
  • Due today, bid desk told
IL-02Make

Near-miss review

13 nodes, 0 decisions.

Watch for

  • The safety rule: no step in these flows, and no Claude step, gives, drafts or changes a switching, clearance, outage or energized-work instruction. Those come from the job brief, the superintendent and system operations, and every task starts with a documented job brief. Storm and emergency calls go to the 24/7 line, (614) 555-0199, answered by people. No flow reads a message for an emergency and no flow dispatches a crew: crews roll within six hours of a call, by a person’s decision.
  • In IL-02 that means Claude never writes a switching, clearance, outage or energized-work step. When a report touches one, Claude sets Touches procedure to Yes and leaves the lesson empty: the safety director writes it, and any change to a procedure goes through the job brief, the superintendents and system operations, never through a bulletin. Every bulletin ends with the same fixed line: switching, clearance and energized-work steps come only from your job brief, your superintendent and system operations.
  • The report form asks what happened, what stopped it being worse and the lesson. It never asks who was at fault, and Near-miss reports has no field for it. Filed by is optional, for the safety director’s questions only: Claude never reads it, and it never reaches a bulletin.
  • Claude reads What happened, What stopped it, The lesson, the discipline and the state, never Filed by or the job number. Its instructions say to write roles, never names (a lineman, the foreman, a crew), and to state what happened without fault. The safety director checks the draft for any name before approving.
  • Only the safety director approves, by setting Status to Approved on the report’s row; Airtable’s field editing permissions let only the safety director, and the flow’s own account, change Status, and the flow only ever sets Awaiting approval and Sent, never Approved. They may rewrite the draft entirely. Nothing goes out before Approved.
  • Make gives a scenario one trigger, so IL-02 is three scenarios in one folder: IL-02 · Near-miss review · draft, · remind and · send. The Automation page draws them as one flow.
  • Review due is an Airtable formula, 48 hours after Filed at, so the promise is measured from the moment a report is filed. Find reviews waiting is an Airtable Make an API call, not a search module, so it always returns one bundle with the list, even an empty one. The filter before Remind the director routes on the list’s length: one or more sends one message listing every report still waiting, and an empty list stops there, with nothing to remind.
  • Bulletin approved watches Status changing, with the formula Status is Approved and Sent at is empty. Mark it reviewed sets Status to Sent, so a later edit to the row never sends a bulletin twice.
  • Draft the bulletin and Read the draft have error routes that post the step and the error, never the report’s text, to /il/failed, then resume with an empty draft and Touches procedure Yes: the report still reaches the safety director, who writes the bulletin by hand. Every module that writes or sends has an error route that posts the step and the error to /il/failed and ends in Break, with incomplete executions stored, so Make keeps the run to retry.

Outcome

Every near-miss filed becomes a draft bulletin within minutes, and goes company-wide within 48 hours of being filed, once the safety director approves it, with no names and no blame.

Trigger

Make’s Airtable Watch Records picks up each report filed on the near-miss form, every 15 minutes. A schedule at 6 a.m. and 2 p.m. Eastern starts the reminder, and a second Watch Records picks up each bulletin the safety director approves.

Exits

  • Sent company-wide, within 48 hours
  • Sent company-wide, after 48 hours
  • Awaiting approval, reminded
IL-03Zapier

Closeout

22 nodes, 1 decision.

Watch for

  • The safety rule: no step in these flows, and no Claude step, gives, drafts or changes a switching, clearance, outage or energized-work instruction. Those come from the job brief, the superintendent and system operations, and every task starts with a documented job brief. Storm and emergency calls go to the 24/7 line, (614) 555-0199, answered by people. No flow reads a message for an emergency and no flow dispatches a crew: crews roll within six hours of a call, by a person’s decision.
  • In IL-03 that means the flow starts after energization and never touches it. Switching is executed with operations by people, and the project lead sets the Energization date only once the line is energized. The package holds the as-builts and test reports as the crew made them, and no step writes in it.
  • As-builts and closeout are turned over within ten business days of energization, as the site says. Closeout due is an Airtable formula, WORKDAY(Energization date, 10), skipping the holidays the office lists in it; Reminder on is the same formula with 8.
  • A Zap has one trigger, so IL-03 is two Zaps: IL-03 · Closeout · clock and IL-03 · Closeout · send. The Automation page draws them as one flow. Paths never rejoin: Name the answer and Record the answer come before them, and each path ends on its own.
  • The ten business days rest on two people: the project lead, who builds the package, and the superintendent, who approves it. On day eight, if the package has not gone, both are reminded. The superintendent’s message says whether a package is waiting for their approval: Find a waiting package searches Closeout packages on Awaiting for, a formula that holds the job number only while the review is Awaiting, and Word the reminder is a Formatter Lookup Table that picks the line. The superintendent hears either way, because a Filter there would also stop the due-day check after it. The flow reminds; it cannot guarantee the turnover.
  • Delay Until waits at most a month, and ten business days never reach it. Each wait is followed by a fresh Check the project and a filter: the clock goes on only while Closeout sent on is empty and the Energization date still matches the one it started with. A corrected date starts its own clock, and the old one stops at its next check, so the lead is reminded once.
  • Human in the Loop notifies the superintendent by email, with the link to its Zapier review page, and records Approved or Rejected. Opening the link, or a mail scanner opening it, records nothing: only the superintendent’s choice on the page does. With no answer, the send Zap goes no further and nothing is sent; the clock still alerts the Projects group on the due day.
  • Name the answer is a Formatter Lookup Table: Approved stays Approved, and anything else becomes Sent back. A sent-back package is never sent: the lead fixes it and submits it again, and each submission is a new row, so it starts its own review.
  • The package is one PDF, under Gmail’s 25 MB limit, as the closeout form asks. The closeout email is a fixed template, and it sends only after the superintendent’s Approved.
  • Check the project is a Find Record with success when nothing is found turned off, so a project deleted mid-clock halts the run. Zapier marks it halted, and a Zapier Manager zap on New Halted Task posts it to /il/failed a few minutes later, with the zap and the time, as Zapier Manager gives them. A failed step, like the email, is caught by a second Zapier Manager zap, on New Zap Error, with the zap, the time and the error’s message. If the email fails, Closeout sent on stays empty, and the lead sends the package by hand once the row shows Approved.

Outcome

Every energized project has its closeout clock running from the day the date is set, the project lead and the superintendent are reminded before it runs out, and the package goes to the owner only after the superintendent approves it. The flow reminds; it cannot guarantee the ten business days, which rest on the package and the superintendent’s approval.

Trigger

Airtable’s New or Updated Record starts the clock Zap when a project’s Energization date is set; Airtable’s New Record starts the send Zap when the project lead submits the closeout package.

Exits

  • Package sent, approved
  • Sent back to the project lead
  • Reminded, then due-day alert
  • No answer, nothing sent

Helpers

Two small workflows the others call.

Why helpers

Alerts and failures are written once, in n8n, and all three platforms report failures to them. Make and Zapier message people with their own Telegram modules.

IL-H01 · Alert a team

Every message n8n sends a team reads the same way and lands in the right chat.

IL-H02 · Log a failed run

Every failed or halted run, on any of the three platforms, is logged and reaches the team that owns the flow with what its platform gives about it.

Five phases, in the order they are built.

Handover

What Ironline keeps.

Written for the bid desk, the safety director and the superintendents

One page, no jargon, for the people who answer bids, review near-misses and close out jobs. It lives next to the flows.

What it does

  • Logs every email to bids@, gives it to the superintendent the bid desk put on bid duty that week, and acknowledges it with the one-business-day promise and the 24/7 line.
  • Tells the bid desk each business morning which requests are due that day and still open.
  • Turns every near-miss report into a draft bulletin with no names, reminds the safety director until it is approved, then sends it company-wide.
  • Starts a closeout clock at energization, reminds the project lead and the superintendent before it runs out, and sends the package to the owner once the superintendent approves it.

What breaks it

  • Adding a step, a template line or a Claude instruction that tells anyone how to switch, clear, take an outage or work a line energized.
  • Adding a field to Near-miss reports for who was at fault, or letting Claude read Filed by.
  • Giving anyone but the safety director, and the flow’s own account, permission to change a report’s Status, or letting the flow set Approved.
  • Renaming a field in any of the six tables, or changing the Status or Superintendent review choices.
  • Letting the Bid duty rota run out of weeks. The bid desk sets it each Friday, which is how a person assigns each week’s superintendent; with no one on it, every request goes to the bid desk by hand.
  • A connection expiring on one platform but not the others.

Who to call

Justine Acosta, who built it. Failures already reach the team that owns the flow on Telegram, with what each platform gives: the step and a link to the run from n8n, the step and the error from Make, the zap and the time from Zapier.

Where the logs are

Every failed or halted run, from any of the three platforms, is a row in the Failed runs tab and a Telegram message. Every bid request keeps who it was assigned to and when it is due, every near-miss when it was filed and sent, and every closeout package who reviewed it and when.
