01

Before anything is built

The outcome, the entry points and every way a request, an invoice or a notice ends.

Why plan first

Riverdale & Brooks makes three promises a flow has to keep: a reply within one business day, hourly matters billed monthly and itemized to the tenth of an hour, and an update from Daniel at every milestone. One rule sits above them all: nothing confidential in any flow. This page pins all of it down before n8n, Make or Zapier is opened.

The legal rule

Nothing confidential enters an automation, Claude, HubSpot or Airtable. The flows carry names, contact details, the area of law and scheduling or billing data only. The few sentences from the form go to Daniel’s inbox alone, the conflict check is done by hand, and messages from a client reach Daniel untouched.

Confidentiality terms

Before any client’s matter goes into these tools, the firm would confirm each vendor’s confidentiality terms and the client’s informed consent, as the profession’s ethics guidance on AI expects.

Outcome

Every consultation request gets the right fee and a booking link within a minute and Ruth knows of it, every open hourly matter’s month is drafted into a Stripe invoice that goes out only on Daniel’s yes, and every client hears where their matter stands when it moves, with nothing confidential in any flow.

Entry points

Seven entry points: Send request on the consultation form, the 1st of each month at 7:00 and at 7:30, a matter’s stage moved in Airtable, RB-01 or RB-H02 calling RB-H01, n8n’s own Error Trigger and a webhook that Make and Zapier report failures to.

Every way a job ends

  • Reply sent, Ruth told
  • Invoice sent on Daniel’s yes
  • Notice sent or with Daniel

Out of scope

  • The few sentences from the form beyond Daniel’s inbox: no table, template, log or Claude ever holds them
  • The conflict-of-interest check, which Daniel or Ruth runs by hand before any engagement letter
  • Opening a matter and the engagement letter, which Ruth and Daniel handle by hand
  • The client portal, its documents and its case updates, which Ruth keeps by hand; no flow reaches it
  • The booking itself, which Cal.com confirms on its own, and collecting the $150 consultation fee, which no flow touches
  • Flat fees, which Daniel bills by hand, and any refund or void, which Daniel makes in Stripe
  • The Practice Q&A bot, the inbox triage agent and the consultation prep agent, planned on the AI agents page
02

Naming convention

One vocabulary across n8n, Make and Zapier.

Why it matters

Three platforms, one attorney and one office manager. When a run fails between two consultations, its name has to say which flow it was, wherever it ran.

Rule of thumb

If a run log does not read like a sentence, rename the steps.

Workflows

RB-[number] · [Title]
RB-01 · Consultation request
RB-H02 · Log a failed run

The same codes on every platform. The number is the build order; helpers get an H.

Scenarios and Zaps

RB-[number] · [Title] · [part]
RB-02 · Monthly invoice · draft
RB-02 · Monthly invoice · tell

A flow a platform has to split keeps one code, and the last word says which part.

Steps

[Verb] [object]
Keep contact and area
Draft the invoice
Send the notice

Default names like “HTTP Request1” or “Router 2” never survive a build.

Webhook paths

/rb/[source]
/rb/consultation
/rb/failed

One path per source, all lowercase.

Connections

RB · [Service] ([platform])
RB · Airtable (n8n)
RB · Gmail Daniel (n8n)
RB · Stripe (Make)

Each platform holds its own connection, and the name says which one to renew. Daniel’s own mailbox has connections of its own, named for him, that only send and draft.

Cal.com event types

[What it is] · 30 min
Estate planning consultation · 30 min
Consultation · 30 min

Each reply links the one that matches its fee.

Airtable tables

Title case, one thing per table
Contacts
Matters
Time entries

Flows read fields by name, never by position, and no table has a field for what a matter is about.

03

Where the data lives

Three Airtable tables, contact, area, scheduling and billing only, and two sheet tabs.

Source of truth

Airtable holds what the flows need: contacts, matters and time. Cal.com holds the calendar, Stripe the invoices, and Google Sheets only keeps logs. What a matter is about stays with Daniel, in his own files, and no flow touches them.

Contact, area, scheduling and billing only

No table has a field for the few sentences from the form, a description of a matter, a party, a note or a document, and none is added later. The handover lists adding one as a break.

Contacts

Name Email Phone Area asked about Consultation fee Requested at Stripe customer Matters

One row per person who asks, matched on email. Area asked about takes the form’s seven choices: Estate planning & probate, Real estate, Family law, Business & agriculture, Civil dispute, Mediation, Something else. Consultation fee is a formula: Free for Estate planning & probate, $150, credited for any other. Stripe customer is the client’s ID in Stripe, which Ruth adds by hand for an hourly client.

Matters

Matter Client Area Billing Hourly rate Stage Step number Stage changed Engagement signed on Daniel adds a line Last notice step Last notice Last notice on Closed on Time entries

One row per matter, opened by Ruth by hand after the consultation, when someone goes ahead. Matter is a formula: the client’s name and the area. Area takes the six practice areas, as the form names them. Billing is Flat or Hourly. Stage is one of the site’s six steps, Consultation, Listen, Assess, Strategy, Representation or Resolution, and Step number is its place, 1 to 6. Stage changed is a Last modified time field that watches Stage only. Engagement signed on is set by Ruth, by hand, once the conflict check is clear and the letter signed. Last notice is Template or With Daniel: which way the last notice went.

Time entries

Matter Date Activity Hours Amount Invoice

One row per entry, logged by Daniel. Activity is one of a fixed list, Meeting, Call, Drafting, Review, Research, Court or Travel, and there is no free-text field. Hours is to one decimal place, the tenth of an hour. Amount is a formula: Hours times the matter’s Hourly rate. Invoice is the Stripe invoice ID, set when the entry is billed.

RB-01n8n

Consultation request

10 nodes, 2 decisions.

Watch for

  • The legal rule: nothing confidential enters an automation, Claude, HubSpot or Airtable. This flow carries names, contact details, the area of law and scheduling or billing data only. The few sentences from the form go to Daniel’s inbox alone, the conflict check is done by hand, and what a matter is about stays with Daniel.
  • Two steps read the few sentences, and only two: Situation written?, which checks whether they are blank, and To Daniel’s inbox, which sends them. Both take them straight from the Webhook step by expression. Keep contact and area has Include Other Input Fields off, so no step after it is handed them.
  • RB-01’s settings save no executions, successful, failed or manual, so n8n keeps no copy of a request or its few sentences, even of a run that fails.
  • To Daniel’s inbox sends from and to daniel@riverdalebrooks.law, on a connection of its own that this one step uses, only to send. The few sentences never reach office@, where the inbox agent sorts mail, and no step summarises them: Daniel reads them as they were typed.
  • Save the contact is a Create or update matched on email, so a second request from the same email updates the same row. There is no search, so nothing can come back empty and end the run quietly.
  • Contacts is saved before any email goes out. If a later step fails, RB-H02 tells Ruth which one, and she answers the newest row in Contacts by hand. If To Daniel’s inbox itself fails, the few sentences go with the run, by design, and Daniel hears them at the consultation instead.
  • Both replies are fixed templates, so they send on their own. They state the cost before booking, as the site promises, and repeat its line: writing to the firm creates no attorney-client relationship, so please don’t send confidential details yet.
  • The two Cal.com event types, Estate planning consultation and Consultation, are 30 minutes, in the office or by video, and ask only for a name and email. Additional notes is hidden, so nothing about a situation enters Cal.com either. Cal.com sends its own booking confirmation.
  • The conflict check is in no flow. Daniel or Ruth runs it by hand, before any engagement letter.

Outcome

Every consultation request is answered within a minute with the right fee and a link to book 30 minutes in the office or by video, Ruth knows who asked, and anything written about a situation reaches Daniel’s inbox alone.

Trigger

n8n’s Webhook catches Request a consultation the moment someone presses Send request.

Exits

  • Free reply sent, Ruth told
  • $150 reply sent, Ruth told
RB-02Make

Monthly invoice

15 nodes, 1 decision.

Watch for

  • The legal rule: nothing confidential enters an automation, Claude, HubSpot or Airtable. This flow carries names, contact details, the area of law and scheduling or billing data only. The few sentences from the form go to Daniel’s inbox alone, the conflict check is done by hand, and what a matter is about stays with Daniel.
  • Time entries holds a date, a kind of work from a fixed list and the hours, with no free-text field. The invoice lines carry the same and an amount, so nothing about what a matter is about reaches Make, Airtable or Stripe.
  • Daniel approves every invoice. Make only creates drafts, with Auto-advance off, so Stripe never finalizes or sends one on its own. Daniel opens each in Stripe, checks it and presses Send invoice; no flow sends an invoice.
  • The email to Daniel links each draft in Stripe’s dashboard. Opening a link records nothing: the only yes is Daniel’s own click on Send invoice, on Stripe’s page.
  • Make gives a scenario one trigger, so RB-02 is two scenarios in one folder: RB-02 · Monthly invoice · draft and RB-02 · Monthly invoice · tell, half an hour apart. The Automation page draws them as one flow.
  • Find hourly matters, Gather time entries and Find the drafts are Make an API call modules, not searches, so each returns one bundle with a list, even an empty one, and the router reads the list’s length. A search that found nothing would end the route and drop the matter without a word.
  • With no open hourly matter, the iterator has nothing to loop over and the run ends; the 7:30 email then says no invoice is waiting. A matter with no time logged passes neither route and ends there: there is nothing to bill.
  • Each entry is marked with its invoice as its line is added, so a second run, after Ruth adds a customer or after Make retries, never bills an entry twice.
  • Flat-fee matters never appear: Find hourly matters asks for Billing = Hourly only. Litigation and contested matters are billed hourly, with Daniel’s written estimate up front, given by hand.
  • Online payment is on Stripe’s own invoice page. A check comes to 114 East Main Street, and Ruth marks the invoice paid in Stripe by hand. No flow refunds or voids an invoice: that is Daniel’s, in Stripe.
  • Every module that writes has an error route that posts the step and the error, never the entries, to /rb/failed, and ends in Break, so Make keeps the run to retry.

Outcome

On the 1st, every open hourly matter’s time is drafted into a Stripe invoice, itemized to the tenth of an hour, and none goes out until Daniel has checked it and pressed Send.

Trigger

Make’s scheduler starts the draft scenario on the 1st of each month at 7:00 am Eastern, and the tell scenario at 7:30.

Exits

  • Drafted, Daniel approves
  • No Stripe customer, with Ruth
  • No time, nothing drafted
RB-03Zapier

Milestone notice

8 nodes, 1 decision.

Watch for

  • The legal rule: nothing confidential enters an automation, Claude, HubSpot or Airtable. This flow carries names, contact details, the area of law and scheduling or billing data only. The few sentences from the form go to Daniel’s inbox alone, the conflict check is done by hand, and what a matter is about stays with Daniel.
  • The notice is a fixed template for each stage: the area of law, the stage, what that step means and what comes next, in the site’s own words. It names nothing about the matter itself. Anything more is Daniel’s line, which he types into his draft in Gmail and sends himself, so it never passes through Zapier or Airtable.
  • Daniel’s line is added by hand in Gmail, not on a Human in the Loop page: a line typed into Zapier would sit in its task history.
  • Zapier paths never join again, so the one step both ways need, Log the notice, comes before the paths, and nothing has to rejoin. The paths read Daniel adds a line from the trigger, as it was when the stage moved, so unticking it first changes nothing.
  • Log the notice runs before anything is sent, so a stage is noticed once even if a step after it fails. A failed send reaches Ruth through RB-H02, and she sends that notice by hand.
  • A Zap has one trigger, and RB-03 is one Zap with one.
  • Clients only goes on only with Engagement signed on set: an attorney-client relationship begins only after the conflict check and a signed engagement letter, and before then nobody gets a matter update. Step number must also be past Last notice step, so a stage moved back, or saved twice, sends nothing.
  • The notices go from daniel@riverdalebrooks.law, so a client’s reply reaches Daniel untouched, never office@, where the inbox agent sorts mail. The connection only sends and drafts; no step reads Daniel’s mail.
  • No flow reaches the client portal. Ruth updates its case updates by hand when she moves the stage.
  • A failed step is caught by a Zapier Manager zap that posts it to /rb/failed.

Outcome

Every time a client’s matter moves forward, the client hears where it stands from Daniel’s own address, as the template or with a line Daniel adds and sends himself.

Trigger

Zapier’s Airtable New or Updated Record hears Stage changed on a matter, a field that changes only when Stage does.

Exits

  • Template sent
  • With Daniel for his line
  • Not a client yet
05

Helpers

Two small workflows the others call.

Why helpers

Alerts and failures are written once, in n8n, and all three platforms report failures to them. Make and Zapier message Ruth and Daniel with their own Telegram modules.

RB-H01 · Alert Ruth

Every message n8n sends Ruth reads the same way and lands in her Telegram chat.

RB-H02 · Log a failed run

Every failed run, on any of the three platforms, is logged and reaches Ruth: a Make or Zapier failure with a link to its run, an RB-01 failure with the workflow and the failed step.

Build order

Five phases, in the order they are built.

Order

Foundation first, then one platform at a time, in the order of their codes: the request on n8n, the monthly invoice on Make, then the milestone notices on Zapier.

  1. 1 · Foundation

    • Create the Airtable tables, Contacts, Matters and Time entries, with contact, area, scheduling and billing fields only
    • Add the connections on each platform: RB · Airtable, RB · Gmail for office@, RB · Gmail Daniel to send and draft only, RB · Telegram, RB · Stripe (test)
    • Create the Sheets tabs: Failed runs, Test matrix
    • Build RB-H01 in n8n, then RB-H02 with RB-01 (not RB-H01) as the covered error workflow

  2. 2 · Requests on n8n

    • Set up the Estate planning consultation and Consultation event types in Cal.com, 30 minutes, office or video, a name and email only, Additional notes hidden
    • Write both reply templates with Daniel and Ruth
    • Build RB-01 with Save failed, Save successful and Save manual executions all set to Do not save, and point Send request at /rb/consultation
    • Run tests t01–t07

  3. 3 · Invoices on Make

    • Set Stripe’s invoice footer and the check address in test mode, and add the test clients as customers
    • Build both RB-02 scenarios, draft and tell, in one Make folder, every writing module with an error route to /rb/failed
    • Show Daniel how to check and send a draft, and Ruth how to mark a check paid
    • Run tests t08–t15 in Stripe test mode

  4. 4 · Notices on Zapier

    • Write the six Where your matter stands templates with Daniel, one for each stage, in the site’s own words
    • Build RB-03, with Log the notice before its two paths
    • Add the Zapier Manager zap that posts failures to /rb/failed
    • Run tests t16–t21 with test matters

  5. 5 · Test and hand over

    • Switch to RB · Stripe (live)
    • Check the legal rule with Daniel: every table, template, view and log holds contact, area, scheduling and billing data only, and the few sentences reach his inbox alone
    • Confirm each vendor’s confidentiality terms before any client’s matter goes in
    • Run the whole matrix again, walk Daniel and Ruth through the handover page, and turn every flow on
08

Handover

What Riverdale & Brooks keeps.

Written for Daniel and Ruth

One page, no jargon, for the attorney and the office manager. It lives next to the flows.

What it does

  • Answers every consultation request within a minute with the right fee and a booking link, and tells Ruth who asked.
  • Sends the few sentences someone writes on the form to Daniel’s inbox, and nowhere else.
  • Drafts every open hourly matter’s month into a Stripe invoice on the 1st, itemized to the tenth of an hour, and lists the drafts for Daniel.
  • Asks Ruth to add a Stripe customer when an hourly client has none.
  • Tells an engaged client where their matter stands each time it moves forward, or puts the notice in Daniel’s drafts for his line.

What breaks it

  • Renaming a field in Contacts, Matters or Time entries, or changing the Area, Stage, Billing, Activity or Last notice choices.
  • Adding a field for the few sentences, a description of a matter or a note to any table, or a notes question to a Cal.com booking page: any of them would let something confidential into a flow.
  • Turning on n8n’s saved executions for RB-01.
  • Switching on Auto-advance for invoices in Stripe: drafts would be sent without Daniel’s yes.
  • Pointing a flow’s email at office@ instead of Daniel’s own address.
  • A connection expiring on one platform but not the others.

Who to call

Justine Acosta, who built it. Failures already reach Ruth on Telegram: a Make or Zapier failure with a link to its run, an RB-01 failure with the workflow and the step that failed.

Where the logs are

Every failed run, from any of the three platforms, is a row in the Failed runs tab and a Telegram message, holding the step and the error and never a client’s data. Every time entry keeps its invoice, and every matter its last notice.
