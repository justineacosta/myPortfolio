Every booking, health report and fleet report, planned before a single step is placed, and not one job started without the owner’s yes.

Three flows and two helpers for Meridian Werks, the independent workshop at Loodsweg 14, Unit B, Rotterdam, open since 2012, with six bays, an alignment cell and seven people. Bookings run on Zapier, health reports on n8n and fleet reports on Make. Airtable holds the job cards, the findings, the reports and the fleet clients, Cal.com the workshop calendar, Telegram every message to the workshop, and Google Sheets the logs. The site promises to confirm by phone within one working day, that no work begins before the findings are documented and approved, that any variation is approved before it happens, and monthly reporting for fleet contracts; each flow keeps one of those promises, and a person keeps each one with it. Claude drafts only the plain-language summary in a health report, rewording what the technician recorded; diagnosis is the workshop’s job. Amounts are in euros, and a registration number stays on the job card.

01

Before anything is built

The outcome, the entry points and every way a booking, a report or a month ends.

Why plan first

Meridian Werks measures before it touches anything, and writes down what it found. This page does the same for the flows: before Zapier, n8n or Make is opened, it pins down who says yes at each step, what Claude may and may not write, and where a registration number is allowed to go.

Who says yes

Ilse Brandt, the workshop manager, confirms every booking by phone, prices every estimate, checks every health report before it reaches the owner’s inbox, and checks every fleet report before it reaches the client. Anton Vosloo signs off every pre-purchase inspection and every finding that is calibration work before its report goes. The owner’s yes, on the answer form or by phone to Ilse, is what starts the work, and a variation needs a new one. The booking receipt, the workshop messages and the fleet report email are fixed templates: the receipt and the messages send on their own, and the fleet report email only once Ilse has checked the report. In these flows Claude drafts only the plain-language summary in a health report, rewording what the technician recorded, and it never diagnoses.

Personal data

A registration number is personal data. It is kept on the job card, and never in a Telegram message, an email subject, a fleet report, a log such as the Failed runs tab, or anything Claude reads. Zapier’s run history holds the booking form’s payload, as it holds every run’s data. The owner’s name, email and phone stay on the job card too, and messages link to the card instead. Amounts are in euros.

Outcome

Every booking is held, carded and receipted within a minute and confirmed by Ilse’s call within one working day, every diagnosed car’s health report reaches its owner checked and priced, with work released only on their yes, and every fleet client gets a checked report on the month.

Entry points

Ten entry points: a booking sent, a working-morning check, a job set to Diagnostics done, a job set to Variation found, the 1st of the month, a fleet report set to Checked, MW-02 or MW-H02 calling MW-H01, n8n’s own Error Trigger and a webhook that Make and Zapier report failures to.

Every way a job ends

  • Booked, receipted and called
  • Approved and released, or Ilse calls
  • Fleet report checked and sent

Out of scope

  • Diagnosing a car: the technicians do it on the lift and the bench, and no flow or Claude step names a fault, a cause or a part of its own
  • Pricing: Ilse sets every fixed estimate by hand, in euros, and no flow or Claude step writes a price
  • Rescheduling and cancelling a booking, which Ilse does by phone or in Cal.com; the Inbox triage agent on the AI agents page sorts those emails
  • Pre-purchase inspections’ 142-point checklist and the road test, which the technicians and Anton do and record by hand
  • Invoicing, the 24-month parts and labour warranty, and RDW periodic inspection work, which the workshop does by hand
  • The Workshop Q&A bot and the two agents, Inbox triage and Weekly report, planned on the AI agents page
02

Naming convention

One vocabulary across Zapier, n8n and Make.

Why it matters

Three platforms and one workshop manager reading every alert. When a run fails, its name has to say which flow and which part it was, wherever it ran.

Rule of thumb

If a run log does not read like a sentence, rename the steps.

Workflows

MW-[number] · [Title]
MW-01 · Service booking
MW-H02 · Log a failed run

The same codes on every platform. The number is the build order; helpers get an H.

Parts

MW-[number] · [Title] · [part]
MW-01 · Service booking · calls due
MW-02 · Health report · one job
MW-03 · Fleet report · send

A flow one Zap, workflow or scenario cannot hold is split into parts in one folder. The Automation page draws them as one flow.

Steps

[Verb] [object]
Hold the slot
Draft the summary
Send the report

Default names like “HTTP Request1” or “Router 2” never survive a build.

Webhook paths

/mw/[source]
/mw/failed

One path per source, all lowercase.

Connections

MW · [Service] ([platform])
MW · Gmail (n8n)
MW · Airtable (Zapier)
MW · Telegram (Make)

Each platform holds its own connection, and the name says which one to renew. Gmail is signed in as workshop@meridianwerks.nl on all three.

Choice fields

Plain words, one fixed list each
Requested, Confirmed, In diagnostics, Diagnostics done, With the owner, Approved, Variation found, Done, Not going ahead
Needed now, Upcoming, Optional
Needed now only, Needed now and upcoming, Everything listed, Not now, Call me first, No answer
To check, Checked, Sent

Stage on Jobs, Priority on Findings, Answer on Reports, and Status on Fleet reports, in that order. The flows, the templates, the forms and this page use the same words.

Airtable tables

Title case, one thing per table
Jobs
Findings
Fleet reports

Flows read fields by name, never by position.

MW-01Zapier

Service booking

11 nodes, 0 decisions.

Watch for

  • A registration number is personal data. It is kept on the job card, and never in a Telegram message, an email subject, a fleet report, a log such as the Failed runs tab, or anything Claude reads. Zapier’s run history holds the booking form’s payload, as it holds every run’s data. The owner’s name, email and phone stay on the job card too, and messages link to the card instead. Amounts are in euros.
  • The site promises “We confirm by phone within one working day.” So the email is a receipt, not a confirmation: it says the request is in, whether the slot asked for is held, and that Ilse will call to confirm within one working day. Ilse makes the call, agrees the date, confirms the booking in Cal.com and sets the card Confirmed. Call by is an Airtable formula, WORKDAY(Received at, 1), skipping the holidays Ilse lists in it. A working day here is Monday to Friday, the phone line’s days, so WORKDAY skips Saturday even though the workshop opens 09:00–14:00 then.
  • A Zap has one trigger, so MW-01 is two Zaps: MW-01 · Service booking · book and MW-01 · Service booking · calls due. The Automation page draws them as one flow. Neither Zap uses Paths: the one value that depends on whether the slot was held, the receipt’s line, comes from a Formatter Lookup Table, so the booking Zap is one straight line.
  • Hold the slot is a Code by Zapier step that posts the booking to Cal.com’s API and returns held yes or no, the booking’s ID and Cal.com’s answer. A slot another owner took a moment earlier is not an error: it returns held no, the card and the workshop message say so, and the receipt says Ilse will agree a time when she calls. Any other answer, like an expired key, also returns held no with Cal.com’s status, which the workshop message shows, so a broken key shows up on the first booking.
  • The Cal.com event type, Workshop visit, has Requires confirmation on, so a held slot is a request until Ilse confirms it, and Cal.com’s own emails to the attendee are turned off for it: the owner hears only the workshop’s receipt, and then Ilse.
  • The booking holds only what the site asks: the vehicle (make, model, year, the registration, optional, mileage in km and the use), the service, the date and the details, where the owner describes the symptom rather than the repair. Diagnosis is the workshop’s job, so no step reads the symptom to decide anything.
  • Find calls due is a Find Many Records with success when nothing is found turned on, so a morning with nothing due goes on to Any to call?, which stops there without a message instead of halting the run.
  • A failed step, like Open a job card, is caught by a Zapier Manager zap on New Zap Error, which posts the zap, the time and the error’s message to /mw/failed, and Ilse gets an urgent alert; the booking stays in the Zap’s history, so she opens the card by hand and calls the owner. A halted run is caught by a second Zapier Manager zap, on New Halted Task, with the zap and the time, as an ordinary alert.

Outcome

The slot is held, the job card is open and the workshop knows before the owner closes the page. The owner has a receipt at once, and Ilse confirms the booking by phone within one working day.

Trigger

Webhooks by Zapier’s Catch Hook starts the booking Zap when the site’s four-step form is sent; Schedule by Zapier starts the calls-due Zap at 07:30 each working morning.

Exits

  • Slot held, receipt sent, Ilse calls
  • Slot taken, receipt sent, Ilse calls
  • Calls due, Ilse told
  • Nothing due
MW-02n8n

Health report

35 nodes, 6 decisions.

Watch for

  • A registration number is personal data. It is kept on the job card, and never in a Telegram message, an email subject, a fleet report, a log such as the Failed runs tab, or anything Claude reads. Zapier’s run history holds the booking form’s payload, as it holds every run’s data. The owner’s name, email and phone stay on the job card too, and messages link to the card instead. Amounts are in euros.
  • The site promises “No work begins before the findings are documented and approved.” The technician documents them; Ilse prices them into a fixed estimate and checks the words; the owner approves. Only the owner’s answer, on the form or by phone to Ilse, sets a job Approved. On the form, Release the work sets it and tells the workshop group. By phone, Ilse records the Answer and Recorded by Ilse at on the report, sets the job Approved by hand and tells the workshop group herself.
  • The site also promises “Any variation is approved before it happens.” A technician who finds work beyond the approved estimate stops that part, records it as a new finding and sets the job to Variation found. That starts MW-02 again, and its report is headed as a variation and holds only the new finding. Until the owner’s new yes, the work already approved goes on and the variation waits. The Automation page draws this as an arc back to Claude’s summary.
  • Variation found starts a run only when Stage changes to it, so every variation ends with the job back at Approved, ready for the next. Release the work sets Approved on the owner’s yes. After Not now, Call me first or No answer, Ilse calls the owner and then sets Stage back to Approved herself, whatever the answer, as the work already approved goes on; a phone yes she records as above. The next variation changes Stage again and starts its own loop. Withdraw the report and Hold the report do the same through Reset the stage: In diagnostics for a health report, Approved for a variation, so setting Diagnostics done or Variation found again starts a new run.
  • Claude never diagnoses. It reads each finding as the technician wrote it, its priority, and the car’s make, model and service, never the registration or the owner’s details. Its instructions say to reword only what is written: no finding, cause, part, price or date of its own. Ilse checks every draft against the technician’s words before it can reach the owner’s inbox.
  • Anton signs off every pre-purchase inspection and every calibration. When the job’s Service is Pre-Purchase Inspection or Performance Refinement, or any finding is ticked Calibration, Anton’s sign-off? sends the report to him first, on a Telegram form: Signed off, or Back to the technician, which withdraws the report, frees its findings for the next one and tells the technician. That gate signs off the findings before they reach the owner; his sign-off on the finished calibration, after the work and its road test, is by hand on the job card, outside these flows.
  • A pre-purchase inspection ends when its report is sent. The buyer does not own the car yet, so there is no work to approve: the email holds the findings, the photographs and any prices Ilse added, with no answer button, Send does not need every finding priced, and Pre-purchase? ends the run with the job Done.
  • The photographs go as attachments. Fetch the photos downloads each finding’s Photos, and Gather the photos puts every file on one item; Send health report’s Attachments field is the expression {{ Object.keys($binary).join() }}, which lists every binary property on that item, whatever Aggregate named it. Gmail refuses a message over 25 MB, so technicians attach at most three photographs per finding, taken at the phone camera’s reduced size, and Ilse removes any extra at her check. A report still over the limit fails after three tries, and MW-H02 tells Ilse, who sends fewer photographs or calls the owner.
  • n8n hands a poll’s records on together, and a Wait holds a whole run. So MW-02 is two parts: MW-02 · Health report · watch holds the two triggers and hands each job on its own, with Execute Sub-workflow set to Run once for each item and Wait for Sub-Workflow Completion off, to MW-02 · Health report · one job. Each job then has its own run, its own searches and its own answer form. The Automation page draws the two parts as one flow.
  • Find new findings has Always Output Data on, so a job set to Diagnostics done with no finding recorded hands an empty item to Any findings?, which tells Ilse and stops. It finds only findings with no report yet, so a variation’s report holds only the new one.
  • Anton signs off and Ilse prices and checks are Telegram’s Send message and wait for response, with a Custom Form, and Limit Wait Time off: n8n keeps the run, waiting at the gate with its data, until the form is sent. The button only opens the form; a link preview or a stray tap records nothing.
  • Owner answers is a Wait node set to resume On Form Submitted. The report’s button links to the run’s own form page, $execution.resumeFormUrl: opening it shows the form and records nothing, and only the form’s submit moves the run on, so a mail scanner that opens the link approves nothing. Limit Wait Time is seven days; n8n keeps the run, with its data, while it waits, and with no answer by then the run goes on as No answer, and Ilse calls.
  • Send or call? reads the report again after Ilse’s answer, so it sends what she left, not what Claude drafted. Send needs every finding priced; a Send with a finding unpriced is held, its findings freed, and Ilse is told why.
  • Draft the summary has On Error set to continue: if Claude fails or times out, the report opens with an empty summary, Ilse’s message says there is no draft, and she writes it herself. Open the report, Send health report and Release the work retry three times; if one still fails, the run fails and MW-H02 alerts Ilse with the step and a link to the run. Both parts of MW-02 keep their failed executions.

Outcome

Every diagnosed car’s findings, photographs and fixed estimate reach the owner as a health report Ilse has checked, and work starts only on the owner’s yes. Any variation goes back to the owner for a new yes before it happens.

Trigger

Two Airtable Triggers in n8n, every minute: a job card set to Diagnostics done, and one set to Variation found.

Exits

  • Approved, the work released
  • Pre-purchase inspection sent, Done
  • Not now, call me first or no answer, Ilse calls
  • Ilse chose to call
  • Not priced, held for Ilse
  • Sent back to the technician
  • No findings, Ilse told
MW-03Make

Fleet report

10 nodes, 0 decisions.

Watch for

  • A registration number is personal data. It is kept on the job card, and never in a Telegram message, an email subject, a fleet report, a log such as the Failed runs tab, or anything Claude reads. Zapier’s run history holds the booking form’s payload, as it holds every run’s data. The owner’s name, email and phone stay on the job card too, and messages link to the card instead. Amounts are in euros.
  • In MW-03 that means a fleet report names each vehicle by the client’s own fleet number and its model, never by registration, and its subject names Meridian Werks, the month and the client only.
  • Ilse checks every report before it is sent. Only Ilse, and the flow’s own account, can change a report’s Status, through Airtable’s field editing permissions, and the flow only ever sets To check and Sent, never Checked. She may correct any line and add a note of her own.
  • Make gives a scenario one trigger, so MW-03 is two scenarios in one folder: MW-03 · Fleet report · draft and MW-03 · Fleet report · send. The Automation page draws them as one flow.
  • Find fleet clients and Gather the month are Airtable Make an API call modules, not search modules, so each always returns one bundle with the list, even an empty one. The filter before Each client routes on the list’s length: one or more goes on, and an empty list stops there, with no client to report. An empty list of jobs is a month with no visits: Jobs is 0, and the report says so.
  • The figures come from the job cards. Days off road is an Airtable formula on Jobs, the working days from In on to Out on; Invoiced is what Ilse invoiced, in euros; Report line is a formula: the date, the fleet number, the model, the work and the cost. Uptime is a formula on Fleet reports: 1 minus Days off road over Vehicles on contract times the month’s working days. Claude writes none of MW-03.
  • Report checked watches Status changing, with the formula Status is Checked and Sent at is empty. Mark it sent sets Status to Sent, so a later edit never sends a report twice.
  • Every module that writes or sends has an error route that posts the step and the error, never a report’s lines, to /mw/failed and ends in Break, with incomplete executions stored, so Make keeps the run to retry. MW-H02 logs it and alerts Ilse, urgently.

Outcome

On the 1st of each month every fleet client’s report of last month’s jobs, days off the road, uptime and costs is drafted for Ilse, and each one goes to the client only after she has checked it.

Trigger

A Make scenario schedule at 06:00 on the 1st of each month drafts the reports; Airtable Watch Records, every 15 minutes, picks up each report Ilse sets to Checked.

Exits

  • Checked and sent
  • Drafted, waiting for Ilse
  • No active clients, nothing drafted
05

Helpers

Two small workflows the others call.

Why helpers

Alerts and failures are written once, in n8n, and all three platforms report failures to them. Zapier and Make message people with their own Telegram modules.

MW-H01 · Alert a person

Every message n8n sends the workshop reads the same way and lands in the right chat.

MW-H02 · Log a failed run

Every failed or halted run, on any of the three platforms, is logged and reaches Ilse with what its platform gives about it.

Handover

What Meridian Werks keeps.

Written for Ilse, Anton and the technicians

One page, no jargon, for the people who book the cars, diagnose them and price the work. It lives next to the flows.

What waits without a nudge

Two things wait with no reminder: a fleet report Ilse has not checked, which stays To check, and a report waiting for Anton’s sign-off, which stays on his Telegram form. Each is told once; Ilse looks through Fleet reports and Reports each Monday for anything still waiting.

What it does

  • Holds the slot for every booking, opens its job card, tells the workshop and sends the owner a receipt, then lists for Ilse each morning the owners she has to call.
  • Turns a technician’s findings into a health report Ilse has priced and checked, with Anton’s sign-off where it is needed, and releases the work only on the owner’s yes.
  • Sends every variation back to the owner for a new yes before the work goes ahead.
  • Drafts each fleet client’s monthly report on the 1st, and sends it once Ilse has checked it.

What breaks it

  • Changing the receipt so it says a booking is confirmed: the site confirms by phone.
  • Letting Claude read the registration or the owner’s details, or adding anything to its instructions that names a fault, a cause or a price.
  • Setting a job Approved without the owner’s answer, or letting technicians start a variation before its new yes.
  • Putting the registration in a Telegram message, an email subject, a fleet report or the Failed runs tab.
  • Renaming a field in any of the six tables, or changing the Stage, Priority, Answer or Status choices.
  • A connection expiring on one platform but not the others, or the Cal.com API key expiring.

Who to call

Justine Acosta, who built it. Failures already reach Ilse on Telegram, with what each platform gives: the zap and the time from Zapier, the step and a link to the run from n8n, the step and the error from Make.

Where the logs are

Every failed or halted run, from any of the three platforms, is a row in the Failed runs tab and a Telegram message. Every job card keeps its booking and when Ilse called, every report who checked it, when it was sent and the owner’s answer, and every fleet report when it was checked and sent.
