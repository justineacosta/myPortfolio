Every site survey, production fault and monthly report, planned before a single step is placed, and not one step that writes the cheque.

Three flows and two helpers for Meridian Energy Infrastructure, which designs, finances, builds and operates commercial solar and battery storage from Houston, San Antonio and Baton Rouge: 184 projects and 312 MW across TX, LA, MS, AL and OK, each carried for its 25 years of operations. Site survey requests from the wizard behind Request a Site Assessment run on Make, production faults on n8n and monthly production reports on Zapier. Google Sheets holds the pipeline and the logs, Airtable holds the assets, the faults, the tickets, the production months and the reports, Cal.com books the drone surveys, and Telegram carries every alert. The site promises that an energy advisor confirms a drone survey window within one business day, a contracted 48-hour response on any production fault, and monthly production reporting measured at the meter and reconciled to utility settlement; each flow keeps one of those promises. Two rules run through them: no step tells a client to touch equipment, and an emergency goes to 911 and the utility; and no step decides or proposes a payment under the production guarantee, which is the asset manager’s call, and no client email names a payment or an amount.

01

Before anything is built

The outcome, the entry points and every way a request, a fault or a report ends.

Why plan first

Meridian Energy Infrastructure operates energized assets for 25 years and guarantees what they produce. A flow that told a client to reset an inverter, or a template that promised a payment, would be worse than no flow at all. This page pins down, before Make, n8n or Zapier is opened, that no step crosses either line, and that every promise on the site has a step that keeps it.

The electrical safety rule

The electrical safety rule: no step, template or message in these flows tells a client to touch, reset, switch or isolate any equipment. Every fault notice tells the client to leave the equipment as it is, and that if they see fire, smoke or damaged equipment, or anyone is hurt, they keep clear and call 911, then their utility. No flow reads SCADA, reads a fault for an emergency, or dispatches a technician: the operations centre logs each fault by hand, and the duty lead dispatches by phone.

The guarantee rule

The guarantee rule: if the asset underperforms the model, Meridian Energy Infrastructure writes the cheque, and whether a payment is due, and how much, is the asset manager’s decision, made by hand. No step, and no Claude step, works out, proposes or promises a payment or an amount, and no client email names a payment or an amount. A month below the model tells the client only that the asset manager reviews it against the contract’s production guarantee; only the asset manager’s own Telegram message reminds them that any payment is their decision.

Who says yes

An energy advisor confirms every survey window and follows up every request the score sends to the guide. The duty lead in the operations centre dispatches every technician. The asset manager approves every monthly report, with its plain-language note, before it reaches a client, and decides any payment under the production guarantee. The booking email, the savings guide, the fault notice and the report email are fixed templates: the first three send on their own, and the report email only once the asset manager has approved the report. In these flows Claude only scores a site survey request; the score routes the email, and the score and Claude’s reason go to the pipeline sheet and the advisors, never to the visitor. The note on each report is drafted by the Report note agent, planned on the AI agents page.

Outcome

Every survey request is scored, logged and in front of an energy advisor within minutes, with a booking link or a guide on its way; every production fault has a ticket, a duty lead and a client notice within a minute; and every asset’s month reaches its client as a report the asset manager has approved.

Entry points

Ten entry points: the wizard’s Book the site survey, a business-morning due check, a fault logged, an hourly 48-hour check, the monthly check on the 10th, the hourly draft passes that act on the 10th, a report approved, ME-02 or ME-H02 calling ME-H01, n8n’s own Error Trigger and a webhook that Make and Zapier report failures to.

Every way a job ends

  • Survey confirmed, or followed up by an advisor
  • Fault responded to within 48 hours
  • Report sent, and any shortfall decided by hand

Out of scope

  • Operating, resetting, switching or isolating any equipment: technicians and the operations centre do that, and no step or template tells a client to
  • Emergencies at a site: 911 and the utility, called by people; no flow reads a fault for an emergency
  • Reading SCADA, a meter or a utility’s settlement data: the operations centre does it and enters what it found by hand; no flow reaches those systems
  • Dispatching a technician: the duty lead, by phone
  • Any payment under the production guarantee: the asset manager decides it, by hand, and it is paid outside these flows
  • The modelled proposal, the interconnection assessment and the PPA pricing that follow a survey: engineering and the capital markets team
  • The Website sales bot and the two agents, Lead research and Report note, planned on the AI agents page
02

Naming convention

One vocabulary across Make, n8n and Zapier.

Why it matters

Three platforms, three teams, and an operations centre that works through the night. When a run fails, its name has to say which flow and which part it was, wherever it ran.

Rule of thumb

If a run log does not read like a sentence, rename the steps.

Workflows

ME-[number] · [Title]
ME-01 · Site survey
ME-H02 · Log a failed run

The same codes on every platform. The number is the build order; helpers get an H.

Parts

ME-[number] · [Title] · [part]
ME-01 · Site survey · due
ME-02 · Production fault · one fault
ME-03 · Production report · send

A flow one scenario, workflow or Zap cannot hold is split into parts in one folder. The Automation page draws them as one flow.

Steps

[Verb] [object]
Score the site
Open a ticket
Send the report

Default names like “HTTP Request1” or “Router 2” never survive a build.

Webhook paths

/me/[source]
/me/failed

One path per source, all lowercase.

Connections

ME · [Service] ([platform])
ME · Gmail (Make)
ME · Airtable (n8n)
ME · Telegram (Zapier)

Each platform holds its own connection, and the name says which one to renew. Gmail is signed in as projects@ on Make, operations@ on n8n and assets@ on Zapier.

Choice fields

Plain words, one fixed list each
Strong fit, Not yet
New, Survey confirmed, Followed up
Open, Dispatched, Responded
To check, Approved, Sent
Payment approved, No payment due

Route and Status on Requests, Status on Tickets, and Status and Guarantee review on Reports, in that order. The flows, the templates and this page use the same words.

Airtable tables

Title case, one thing per table
Faults
Tickets
Production months

Flows read fields by name, never by position.

03

Where the data lives

A pipeline sheet for the surveys, six Airtable tables for the assets, and a log.

Source of truth

Google Sheets holds the pipeline, the general page’s pipeline sheet, and the logs. Airtable holds the team, the assets, the faults and their tickets, the production months and the reports. Cal.com holds the survey bookings.

One vocabulary per field

Every choice field takes one fixed list, and the flows, the templates and this page use the same words for it.

No amount anywhere

Reports has no field for a payment amount. Guarantee review records only the asset manager’s decision, and any payment is made outside these flows.

Requests

Received at Reference Facility Area Monthly spend Location Structure Name Organisation Email Score Reason Route Confirm by Status

A tab in the Pipeline spreadsheet in Google Sheets: one row per survey request, added by ME-01. Score and Reason are Claude’s, and stay here and in the advisors’ chat. Route is Strong fit or Not yet. Confirm by is the next business day, written by Make, skipping weekends but not holidays. Status is New, Survey confirmed or Followed up, set by the advisors. The Due tab is one FILTER formula over this tab, inside IFERROR, so it is empty when nothing is due. Each row is one request, identified by Received at and its row; Reference is the friendly label the wizard showed, sized from the area, and two requests can share it.

Team

Name Email Role Telegram chat ID

Everyone the flows name, one row per email. Role is Energy advisor, Duty lead, Asset manager or Field technician. Advisors, duty leads and asset managers start the company’s Telegram bot once, so the flows can message them. Field technicians are listed for the ticket’s Technician field and have no chat ID: no step messages a technician.

Assets

Asset Asset ID Client Site State Asset manager Asset manager chat ID Notice email Report email

One row per operating asset. Notice email is the address the client names for fault notices, and Report email the one for monthly reports. Asset manager links to Team, and the chat ID is a lookup through it.

Faults

Logged at Asset Detected at What they saw Logged by

One row per production fault, logged by hand by the operations centre from what its SCADA shows. Logged at is a Created time field. No flow writes here.

Tickets

Fault Asset Detected at Respond by Warn at Status Technician Dispatched at Notice sent at Warned at Responded at Within 48 hours

One row per fault, opened by ME-02. Respond by is Detected at plus 48 hours, Warn at is Respond by less 12 hours, and Within 48 hours a formula. Status is Open, Dispatched or Responded; the duty lead sets Dispatched, Technician, Dispatched at, Responded and Responded at.

Production months

Asset Month Metered kWh Modelled kWh Reconciled Reconciled by Report

One row per asset per month, entered by the operations centre: Metered kWh from the revenue-grade meter, reconciled to utility settlement, and Modelled kWh, the month’s P50 from the asset’s production model. Report is the other side of the link from Reports. The Not reconciled and Report due views feed ME-03.

Reports

Production month Asset Client Month Metered kWh Modelled kWh Difference Below model Note draft Note Status Status changed Status set by Approved by Sent on Guarantee review

One row per asset per month, drafted by ME-03 or by hand. Asset, Client, Month and both kWh fields are lookups through Production month; Difference and Below model are formulas. The Report note agent writes Note draft, and the asset manager writes Note. Status is To check, Approved or Sent, and only the asset managers, and the Zaps’ own account, may change it; Status changed is a Last modified time field on Status, and Status set by a Last modified by field on Status; Mark it sent copies Status set by, as Report approved read it, into Approved by before it sets Sent, so the approver’s name is kept. Guarantee review is Payment approved or No payment due, set by the asset manager by hand for a month below the model, and empty otherwise.

ME-01Make

Site survey

13 nodes, 1 decision.

Watch for

  • The wizard tells every visitor who books: “An energy advisor will confirm a drone survey window within one business day.” So every request reaches the energy advisors’ group before the visitor’s email goes, whatever the score. Confirm by is the next business day, worked out by Make when the row is added: it skips weekends, not holidays, so around a holiday an advisor on cover answers the day’s requests. A strong site books its window on Cal.com and an advisor confirms it; for the rest, an advisor reads the site and calls or writes within the day, and may offer a window after all.
  • Claude scores, and the score only routes. Score the site reads the kind of facility, the area, the spend and the location, never the name, the organisation or the email. Its instructions give the company’s minimum site, 80,000 sq ft or 250 kW, the size range the wizard shows for each kind of facility, and the five markets, and ask for a whole number from 1 to 10 and one line on why. The score and the reason go to the pipeline sheet and the advisors’ message. Neither email holds either, and Claude writes no part of any email.
  • Strong fit is a score of 7 or more, the general page’s line. Not yet is the Router’s fallback route, so a score below 7, or no score at all, goes to the guide. A visitor never hears that their site scored low: the guide is the same for every site of its kind, and an advisor follows up in person.
  • The two emails are fixed templates from projects@meridianenergy.com, the address the site gives. Book the survey holds the link to Cal.com’s Drone site survey event, which has Requires confirmation on, so the window the visitor picks waits for an advisor, and Cal.com sends its own confirmation once they confirm. The savings guide is one of five, one per kind of facility in the wizard, picked in the email with Make’s switch() function. Neither calls the assessment free, and neither holds a figure for the visitor’s site: the wizard’s preliminary model is the visitor’s own, and the survey replaces it with measured values.
  • Make gives a scenario one trigger, so ME-01 is two scenarios in one folder: ME-01 · Site survey · request and ME-01 · Site survey · due. The Automation page draws them as one flow.
  • Find requests due is a Google Sheets Make an API call, not a search module, so it always returns one bundle, even when nothing is due. It reads the Due tab, whose one formula, in A2, is a FILTER over Requests (Status is New, and Confirm by is today or earlier) wrapped in IFERROR with an empty result. FILTER alone shows #N/A when nothing matches, which the API would return as one row; with the IFERROR, a day with nothing due leaves the tab empty, and the API leaves out the values list. The filter before Remind the advisors routes on the length of the values it returns: one or more sends one message listing every request still due, and an empty tab stops there, with nothing to remind.
  • Score the site and Read the score have error routes that post the step and the error, never the visitor’s answers or details, to /me/failed, then resume with an empty score and the reason No score: the request is still logged, the advisors are told there is no score, and the visitor still gets the guide and an advisor’s follow-up. Every module that writes or sends has an error route that posts the step and the error to /me/failed and ends in Break, with incomplete executions stored, so Make keeps the run to retry. ME-H02 logs it and alerts the advisors, urgently.

Outcome

Strong sites get a survey on the calendar; the rest get a useful guide instead of silence. An energy advisor sees every request at once: a strong site’s survey window is confirmed within one business day, as the wizard promises, and for the rest an advisor is in touch within one business day.

Trigger

Make’s custom webhook catches the site survey wizard when the visitor presses Book the site survey; a scenario schedule at 8:00 a.m. Central, Monday to Friday, starts the due check.

Exits

  • Strong fit, booking link sent, advisor confirms
  • Not yet, guide sent, advisor follows up
  • No score, guide sent, advisors told
  • Due today, advisors reminded
  • Nothing due
ME-02n8n

Production fault

17 nodes, 2 decisions.

Watch for

  • The electrical safety rule: no step, template or message in these flows tells a client to touch, reset, switch or isolate any equipment. Every fault notice tells the client to leave the equipment as it is, and that if they see fire, smoke or damaged equipment, or anyone is hurt, they keep clear and call 911, then their utility. No flow reads SCADA, reads a fault for an emergency, or dispatches a technician: the operations centre logs each fault by hand, and the duty lead dispatches by phone.
  • In ME-02 that means the notice opens with the emergency line, so an emergency reads as one: if they see fire, smoke or damaged equipment, or anyone is hurt, they keep clear and call 911, then their utility. Only then does it give the 48-hour response, and it tells the client to leave all equipment as it is, never to touch, reset or isolate anything themselves. Nothing in ME-02 reads a fault for urgency or decides how to make an asset safe: that is the operations centre’s and the technician’s work, and an emergency at a site is a call to 911 and the utility, made by people.
  • The site promises “a contracted 48-hour response on any production fault.” That is a response, not a repair. Respond by is an Airtable formula, 48 hours after Detected at, the time the operations centre saw the fault begin. Responded at is when a technician is working the fault, set by the duty lead, and Within 48 hours is a formula. The notice says Meridian Energy Infrastructure responds within 48 hours, by Respond by; it never promises a repair, a time on site, or when production returns.
  • The operations centre watches every asset on its 24/7 SCADA, and a person logs each fault in Faults by hand, choosing the asset from Assets. No step reads SCADA, a meter or any other system of the operations centre’s, and no step reaches a utility.
  • A person dispatches every technician. Tell the duty lead messages the operations group; the duty lead picks the technician on call, calls them and sets Status to Dispatched, with Technician and Dispatched at. No step picks, messages or sends a technician.
  • n8n hands a poll’s records on together, and a search’s Always Output Data speaks for the whole batch, not for each record. So ME-02 is two parts: ME-02 · Production fault · watch holds the two triggers and hands each fault on its own, with Execute Sub-workflow set to Run once for each item, to ME-02 · Production fault · one fault. Every search there sees one fault, so an empty result means that fault. The Automation page draws the two parts as one flow.
  • Find the contact has Always Output Data on, so an asset with no notice contact hands an empty item to Contact on file?, which sends it to ME-H01: the operations group is told at once, with sound, and the duty lead tells the client by phone and adds the contact to the asset.
  • Every hour, Find tickets near due has Always Output Data on. Each ticket with no Responded at whose Warn at, 12 hours before Respond by, has passed, and that has not been warned, goes to Mark it warned, which sets Warned at so it is warned once, and then to ME-H01, which warns the operations group with sound. An empty result goes to Nothing near due.
  • The notice is a fixed template from operations@meridianenergy.com, with Append n8n Attribution off. Claude writes none of ME-02.
  • Open a ticket, Tell the duty lead and Notify the client retry three times. If one still fails, the run fails and ME-H02 alerts the operations group with the step and a link to the run. Both parts of ME-02 keep their failed executions, so a person reads the fault there, or in Faults, and finishes by hand.

Outcome

Every production fault the operations centre logs opens a ticket within a minute, the duty lead hears at once, and the client gets a notice that Meridian Energy Infrastructure responds within 48 hours. A person dispatches the technician, and the flow warns the operations centre before the 48 hours run out.

Trigger

n8n’s Airtable Trigger checks Faults every minute for a fault the operations centre has logged; a Schedule Trigger runs the 48-hour check every hour.

Exits

  • Ticket open, client notified, the duty lead dispatches
  • No notice contact, the duty lead calls the client
  • Responded within 48 hours
  • Warned 12 hours before Respond by
  • Nothing near due
ME-03Zapier

Production report

21 nodes, 0 decisions.

Watch for

  • The guarantee rule: if the asset underperforms the model, Meridian Energy Infrastructure writes the cheque, and whether a payment is due, and how much, is the asset manager’s decision, made by hand. No step, and no Claude step, works out, proposes or promises a payment or an amount, and no client email names a payment or an amount. A month below the model tells the client only that the asset manager reviews it against the contract’s production guarantee; only the asset manager’s own Telegram message reminds them that any payment is their decision.
  • In ME-03 that means a report holds the month’s figures and a Below model flag, both from Airtable formulas, and nothing else about the guarantee. Word the line and Word the month are Formatter Lookup Tables with fixed lines: a month below the model tells the asset manager that any payment is theirs to decide, and tells the client only that the asset manager reviews it against the contract’s production guarantee. Guarantee review is set by the asset manager alone, Payment approved or No payment due, and Reports has no field for an amount. Any payment is made outside these flows, on the asset manager’s decision.
  • Measured at the meter, not the nameplate, as the site says. The operations centre enters each asset’s month in Production months by hand: the generation read from the revenue-grade meter and reconciled to the utility’s settlement data, and then it ticks Reconciled. Modelled kWh is that month’s P50 from the asset’s production model. No step reads a meter, SCADA or a utility’s system.
  • A Zap has one trigger, so ME-03 is three Zaps: ME-03 · Production report · check, · draft and · send. The Automation page draws them as one flow. None uses Paths: the one value that depends on whether a month came in below the model, the line, comes from a Formatter Lookup Table, so each Zap is one straight line.
  • Find months not ready is a Find Many Records with success when nothing is found turned on, so the asset managers hear either way: Name what’s missing is a Formatter Default Value that says none when every month is reconciled. The search returns at most 100 months, so the message always links the Not reconciled view, which lists every one.
  • Airtable’s Find Many Records returns at most 100 records, and Meridian Energy Infrastructure operates 184 assets. So the draft Zap runs every hour and acts only on the 10th, from 8:00 a.m. Central: Read the day and Read the hour are Formatter Date / Time steps on the run’s own time, taken from its UTC timestamp with From Timezone UTC and converted to Central time, so the account’s own timezone cannot skew it, and On the 10th filters on them, not on the schedule’s own fields. Each pass takes up to 100 months from the Report due view, reconciled with no report yet, and a month leaves the view once its report is drafted, so none gets two. Find metered months has success when nothing is found turned on, and Any to report? stops a pass with nothing left, quietly, without a halted run. Looping by Zapier runs each month after it as its own pass.
  • A month reconciled after the 10th has no report from that day’s passes. The 7:00 a.m. check has already named it, and the asset manager drafts its report by hand on a new Reports row, linked to the month, which the send Zap sends like any other once it is Approved.
  • Only the asset manager approves, by setting Status to Approved on the report’s row; Airtable’s field editing permissions let only the asset managers, and the Zaps’ own account, change Status, and the Zaps only ever set To check and Sent, never Approved. The plain-language note is drafted into Note draft by the Report note agent, planned on the AI agents page; the asset manager edits it into Note, or writes it, before approving.
  • Report approved watches Status changing, and Approved, not sent goes on only when Status is Approved and Sent on is empty. Mark it sent sets Status to Sent, so a later edit to the row never sends a report twice.
  • A failed step, like Send the report, is caught by a Zapier Manager zap on New Zap Error, which posts the zap, the time and the error’s message to /me/failed, and the asset managers get an urgent alert; Sent on stays empty, and the asset manager sends the report by hand once the row shows Approved. A halted run is caught by a second Zapier Manager zap, on New Halted Task, with the zap and the time, as an ordinary alert.

Outcome

Each month every client’s asset has a report of its generation, measured at the meter and reconciled to utility settlement, against the model, and it reaches the client only once the asset manager approves it. Any payment under the production guarantee is the asset manager’s decision, made by hand.

Trigger

Schedule by Zapier starts the check Zap at 7:00 a.m. Central on the 10th of each month, and the draft Zap every hour, acting only on the 10th; Airtable’s New or Updated Record starts the send Zap when the asset manager sets a report Approved.

Exits

  • Approved and sent
  • Below the model, sent, shortfall decided by hand
  • Not reconciled, asset managers told
  • Nothing to report

Helpers

Two small workflows the others call.

Why helpers

Alerts and failures are written once, in n8n, and all three platforms report failures to them. Make and Zapier message people with their own Telegram modules.

ME-H01 · Alert a team

Every message n8n sends a team reads the same way and lands in the right chat.

ME-H02 · Log a failed run

Every failed or halted run, on any of the three platforms, is logged and reaches the team that owns the flow with what its platform gives about it.

Handover

What Meridian Energy Infrastructure keeps.

Written for the advisors, the operations centre and the asset managers

One page, no jargon, for the people who answer survey requests, run the operations centre and look after the assets. It lives next to the flows.

What it does

  • Scores and logs every survey request, tells the energy advisors at once, and sends a strong site a booking link and the rest a guide, with an advisor following up every one within one business day.
  • Opens a ticket for every fault the operations centre logs, tells the duty lead, sends the client the 48-hour notice, and warns the operations group 12 hours before any Respond by with no response.
  • Drafts every reconciled month’s report on the 10th, tells the asset manager, and sends it once they approve it.
  • Tells the asset managers on the 10th which months are not reconciled, so none goes without a report unseen.

What breaks it

  • Adding a step, a template line or a Claude instruction that tells a client to touch, reset, switch or isolate any equipment, or that drops the 911 and utility line from the notice.
  • Adding a field for a payment amount to Reports, any step that works out, proposes or promises a payment, or any client email line that names a payment or an amount.
  • Letting Claude read a visitor’s name, organisation or email, or putting the score or the reason in an email.
  • Giving anyone but the asset managers, and the Zaps’ own account, permission to change a report’s Status, or letting a Zap set Approved.
  • Renaming a field in any table, or changing the Route, Status or Guarantee review choices.
  • A connection expiring on one platform but not the others.

Who to call

Justine Acosta, who built it. Failures already reach the team that owns the flow on Telegram, with what each platform gives: the step and the error from Make, the step and a link to the run from n8n, the zap and the time from Zapier.

Where the logs are

Every failed or halted run, from any of the three platforms, is a row in the Failed runs tab and a Telegram message. Every survey request keeps its score, its route and when it was answered, every ticket when it was detected, dispatched and responded to, and every report who approved it and when it was sent.
