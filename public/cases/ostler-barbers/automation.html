What happens after someone books a chair at Ostler

Ostler’s site has no form. Its calls to action are buttons: Book with Tomas, Jonah or Arun opens that barber’s own Cal.com page, Book a chair opens a short chooser of all four barbers’ pages, and Become a member and Add to bag end on Stripe’s own checkout pages. So these flows start from a Cal.com booking or a Stripe payment. Each barber keeps their own book: every barber has a Cal.com account of their own, with one event type for each service they offer, so a booking lands in that barber’s book and carries their name into the flow. Leo, the apprentice, has his own page too, in the chooser and beside the site’s line on apprentice cuts, for chair four on Tuesdays and Wednesdays. Every email in these three flows is a fixed template that sends on its own, and none of them uses Claude. A barber approves any note Claude drafts for a client’s inbox before it is sent, as the AI agents page shows. Money is paid on Stripe’s own pages, and any refund is made by a person, by hand, in Stripe. Pick a flow, then open any step to see what it does, what it reads and why it is there.
