01

Before anything is built

The outcome, the entry points and every way a client’s story ends.

Why plan first

Two of Altitude’s promises are about time, a reply within two working days and every check-in answered by Monday, and one is about money: nothing to sign until you have met your coach. This page pins all three down before n8n, Make or Zapier is opened.

Outcome

Every applicant is offered the free consult and Elias is briefed, every plan starts only after Elias marks the consult Starting, and every Sunday check-in reaches Elias with a summary, with a nudge for anyone who missed it.

Entry points

Eight entry points: the application behind Start coaching, a consult marked Starting in Airtable, a Stripe checkout paid or expired, a check-in form, a schedule on Sunday night, AL-01 or AL-H02 calling AL-H01, n8n’s own Error Trigger and a webhook that Make and Zapier report failures to.

Every way a job ends

  • Started
  • Not now
  • Link expired
  • Check-in answered

Out of scope

  • The coaching app: sessions, messages and Elias’s replies live there, and no flow reads or writes it
  • Book a consult, which opens Elias’s Cal.com page directly
  • Pausing or cancelling a plan, which Elias does in Stripe by hand
  • The Program Q&A bot, form review and block review, planned on the AI agents page
02

Naming convention

One vocabulary across n8n, Make and Zapier.

Why it matters

Three platforms, one coach. When a run fails on a Sunday night, its name has to say which flow it was, wherever it ran.

Rule of thumb

If a run log does not read like a sentence, rename the steps.

Workflows

AL-[number] · [Title]
AL-01 · Coaching application
AL-H02 · Log a failed run

The same codes on every platform. The number is the build order; helpers get an H.

Scenarios and Zaps

AL-[number] · [Title] · [part]
AL-02 · Start a plan · paid
AL-03 · Sunday check-in · missed

A flow a platform has to split keeps one code, and the last word says which part.

Steps

[Verb] [object]
Check the roster
Create the checkout
Send a nudge

Default names like “HTTP Request1” or “Router 2” never survive a build.

Webhook paths

/al/[source]
/al/apply
/al/failed

One path per source, all lowercase.

Connections

AL · [Service] ([platform])
AL · Airtable (n8n)
AL · Airtable (Make)
AL · Gmail (Zapier)

Each platform holds its own connection, and the name says which one to renew.

Stripe keys

AL · Stripe ([mode])
AL · Stripe (test)
AL · Stripe (live)

Test and live keys never share a name, and going live is the last task.

Airtable tables

Title case, one thing per table
Applications
Plans
Check-ins

Flows read fields by name, never by position.

Four Airtable tables, four stages and two sheet tabs.

Source of truth

Airtable holds what Elias edits: applications, plans, clients and check-ins. Stripe holds the subscriptions, Cal.com the calendar, and Google Sheets only keeps logs. The coaching app holds the training itself, and no flow touches it.

AL-01n8n

Coaching application

10 nodes, 1 decision.

Watch for

  • The application asks what the site says it asks: your goal, your schedule and what you have tried before. Its only other field is the email the reply goes to.
  • Book a consult and Book a call with Elias skip the application and open Elias’s Cal.com consult page, so AL-01 never runs for them. If that person starts, Elias adds their row to Applications by hand.
  • The reply is a fixed template, so it sends without waiting for anyone, well inside the two working days the site promises. Claude’s summary only ever reaches Elias.
  • In n8n, a search that finds nothing ends the run quietly. Check the roster has Always Output Data on, so an email on no client’s row goes on as New instead of stopping.
  • Applicants are matched on email, never on name. A second application from the same email updates the same row.
  • Claude gives no training or injury advice. An injury in the answers is flagged for the consult, where Elias asks about it in person.
  • If Claude fails, the person already has their reply. The run stops with an error, and AL-H02 tells Elias, who reads the answers himself.

Outcome

Every new applicant is offered the free 20-minute consult within minutes, well inside the two working days the site promises, and Elias has read a summary of their goal before the call.

Trigger

The application behind Start coaching posts to /al/apply.

Exits

  • Consult offered, Elias briefed
  • A client, with Elias
AL-02Make

Start a plan

19 nodes, 2 decisions.

Watch for

  • Nothing is charged before Elias marks the consult Starting. That is his yes, and the client then starts the subscription on Stripe’s own page, with their own card.
  • Make gives a scenario one trigger, so AL-02 is two scenarios in one folder: AL-02 · Start a plan · link ends when the payment link is emailed, and Stripe’s event starts AL-02 · Start a plan · paid. The diagram draws them as one flow, and the application’s Status joins them.
  • Watch Records needs a Created or Last modified time field, so Applications has Status or plan changed, a Last modified time field that watches Status and Plan. Its formula takes only rows at Starting with a plan chosen. If Elias sets Starting before he picks the plan, the row waits in the Starting, no plan view, and picking the plan changes the field again, so the next check takes it.
  • A Stripe Checkout link lasts 24 hours. An unpaid one sends checkout.session.expired, the row goes to Link expired, and Elias follows up. Setting it back to Starting sends a fresh link.
  • Stripe can send an event twice. Find the application is a Get a record by the ID the checkout carries, not a search, so it always returns one bundle, and both routes require Awaiting payment. A repeat matches neither route and stops: one client, one booking.
  • Stripe bills monthly from the day the client pays. The 12-week first block and the 14 days’ notice after it are terms in the emails, not Stripe settings, and any pause or cancel is done by Elias in Stripe by hand.
  • The payment email and both welcomes are fixed templates, with no words from Claude.
  • Cal.com’s own confirmation email for the assessment event types is turned off, or replaced by the welcome, so the client has one email to read.
  • Every module that writes has an error route that posts to /al/failed and ends in Break, so Make keeps the run to retry.

Outcome

Once Elias marks a consult Starting, the client starts the plan they chose on Stripe, has the 60-minute assessment held and a welcome in their inbox, and nothing is charged before Elias’s yes.

Trigger

Make’s Airtable module watches Applications every 15 minutes for a row Elias has set to Starting. Stripe’s paid or expired checkout starts its second scenario.

Exits

  • Started, assessment held
  • Started, Elias books the hour
  • Link expired, with Elias
  • Waiting on the client
AL-03Zapier

Sunday check-in

12 nodes, 0 decisions.

Watch for

  • Elias writes every reply himself. Claude’s summary goes to him alone and never becomes the reply, so there is no approval step: nothing Claude writes is on its way to a client.
  • No flow reads or writes the coaching app. The check-in arrives on an Airtable form whose link is in the client’s welcome email, and Elias types his reply in the app himself, then marks the check-in Replied.
  • A Zap has one trigger, so AL-03 is two Zaps: AL-03 · Sunday check-in · received and AL-03 · Sunday check-in · missed. The Automation page draws them as one trigger with two ways out, Check-in sent and None by Sunday, one for each Zap; there is no decision step. A check-in sent after a nudge is a new run of the received Zap, drawn there as Sent after the nudge. Nothing waits for it.
  • Each client’s form link is pre-filled with their own row, and hides it, so a check-in always lands on the right client.
  • Read the past weeks counts nothing found as a success, so a first check-in is still summarised, with nothing to compare.
  • Claude gives no training, nutrition or injury advice. Pain or an injury in the answers is flagged first in the summary, for Elias.
  • The nudge is a fixed template that asks for the check-in and nothing else, so it sends on its own.
  • A client before their block start, or Paused, is never nudged: the Check-in missing view leaves them out.
  • A failed step is caught by a Zapier Manager zap that posts it to /al/failed.

Outcome

Every Sunday check-in reaches Elias with a summary beside it, so he can write each reply himself by Monday, and anyone who has not checked in by Sunday night gets a nudge.

Trigger

Airtable’s New Record on Check-ins starts one Zap; Schedule by Zapier, every Sunday at 9 pm Mountain time, starts the other.

Exits

  • Summarised, Elias replies
  • Nudged
  • Everyone checked in
05

Helpers

Two small workflows the others call.

Why helpers

Alerts and failures are written once, in n8n, and all three platforms report failures to them. Make and Zapier message Elias with their own Telegram modules.

AL-H01 · Alert Elias

Every message n8n sends Elias reads the same way and lands in his Telegram chat.

AL-H02 · Log a failed run

Every failed run, on any of the three platforms, is logged and reaches Elias with a link to it.

Build order

Five phases, in the order they are built.

Order

Foundation first, then one platform at a time, in the order a client meets them: the application, the start, then the first Sunday.

  1. 1 · Foundation

    • Create the Airtable tables: Applications, Plans, Clients, Check-ins, with the Check-in missing and Starting, no plan views
    • Add the connections on each platform: AL · Airtable, AL · Gmail, AL · Anthropic, AL · Telegram, AL · Cal.com, AL · Stripe (test)
    • Create the Sheets tabs: Failed runs, Test matrix
    • Build AL-H01 in n8n, then AL-H02 with AL-01 (not AL-H01) as the covered error workflow

  2. 2 · Applications on n8n

    • Set up Elias’s Consult event type in Cal.com, 20 minutes
    • Build AL-01 and point the application behind Start coaching at /al/apply
    • Run tests t01–t06

  3. 3 · Starts on Make

    • Create the three monthly prices in Stripe test mode, and fill Plans with them
    • Set up the Assessment event types in Cal.com, studio and video, 60 minutes, with their own confirmation emails turned off, or replaced by the welcome
    • Build both AL-02 scenarios, the link and the paid, in one Make folder
    • Give every module that writes an error route to /al/failed
    • Run tests t07–t13 with test applicants and Stripe test cards

  4. 4 · Check-ins on Zapier

    • Build the check-in form on Check-ins, and the pre-filled Check-in form formula on Clients
    • Build both AL-03 Zaps, received and missed
    • Add the Zapier Manager zap that posts failures to /al/failed
    • Run tests t14–t20 with test clients

  5. 5 · Test and hand over

    • Switch AL-02 to AL · Stripe (live) and its live prices
    • Run the whole matrix again with Elias
    • Walk Elias through the handover page
    • Turn every flow on before a Sunday
08

Handover

What Altitude keeps.

Written for Elias

One page, no jargon, for the person who reads every check-in. It lives next to the flows.

What it does

  • Offers every new applicant the free 20-minute consult within minutes, and gives Elias a summary of their goal.
  • Sends a current client’s application straight to Elias.
  • Starts a plan only after Elias marks the consult Starting: the payment link first, then, once paid, the client’s row, the assessment and the welcome.
  • Tells Elias when a payment link lapses, and when a new client needs setting up in the app, with a reminder to enter their Block start, which the Sunday nudge reads.
  • Puts a summary beside every Sunday check-in for Elias, who writes each reply himself.
  • Nudges every active client who has not checked in by Sunday night.

What breaks it

  • Renaming a field in Applications, Plans, Clients or Check-ins.
  • Changing the Status choices in Applications, which AL-02 watches.
  • Changing a price in Stripe without changing the Stripe price in Plans.
  • Editing the check-in form’s questions, or the Check-in missing view.
  • Changing the Consult or Assessment event types in Cal.com.
  • A connection expiring on one platform but not the others.

Who to call

Justine Acosta, who built it. Failures already reach Elias on Telegram with a link to the run.

Where the logs are

Every failed run, from any of the three platforms, is a row in the Failed runs tab and a Telegram message. Every application keeps its status in Airtable, every client their Stripe subscription, and every check-in its summary and whether it was answered.
