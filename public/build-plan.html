02

Naming convention

One vocabulary for workflows, nodes, links, keys and fields.

Why it matters

Six workflows today, thirty next year. Names are how the owner finds the one that broke.

Rule of thumb

If an execution log does not read like a sentence, rename the nodes.

Workflows

TM-[number] · [Trigger] → [Outcome]
TM-01 · Quote request → Qualified lead
TM-H02 · Notify ops

The number is the build order. Helpers get an H.

Nodes

[Verb] [object]
Find contact
Draft estimate
Log the quote

Default names like “HTTP Request1” never survive a build.

Webhook paths

/tm/[source]
/tm/quote
/tm/change
/tm/stripe

One path per source, all lowercase.

Credentials

TM · [Service] ([mode])
TM · Stripe (live)
TM · Stripe (test)

Test and live keys never share a name.

Supabase fields

snake_case, _at for times, _usd for money
deposit_paid_at
estimate_usd

The suffix tells you the unit.

Sheets tabs

Title Case, one job per tab
Crew schedule
Rate card

Workflows read columns by header name, never by position.

Where the data lives

Three tables, seven stages and five sheet tabs.

Source of truth

Supabase holds jobs and contacts. Sheets hold what people edit by hand: the rates and the crew schedule.

contacts

id name email phone created_at

One row per person, matched on email or phone.

jobs

id contact_id stage move_date size from_zip to_zip estimate_usd deposit_paid_at crew nudges_sent source

One row per move.

events

job_id type payload at

Every workflow writes one, so the next run knows what already happened.

Job stages

Sheets tabWhat it holds
Quote logEvery request, as it arrived

Crew scheduleOne row per booked move. The crew edits it.
Test matrixThe test rows below, with results

TM-01

Quote request → Qualified lead

14 nodes, 2 decisions.

Watch for

  • Claude returns JSON only. The parser rejects anything else, and TM-H01 catches it.
  • Duplicates are matched on email or phone, never on name.
  • The question never links to a form.

Outcome

Every quote becomes a job, or one clear question, within 5 minutes.

Trigger

The quote form on the site posts to /tm/quote.

Exits

  • Merged into an open job
  • Waiting on one answer
  • Survey link sent
TM-02

Survey → Estimate sent

15 nodes, 2 decisions.

Watch for

  • Nothing reaches the client until the owner approves.
  • Rate card changes apply to new estimates only.
  • Reminders respect business hours in Central time.

Outcome

Every survey ends with an approved estimate and a deposit link, the same day.

Trigger

Cal.com posts BOOKING_CREATED to /tm/survey.

Exits

  • Estimate sent
  • Sent back to the owner
  • No-show, rebook link sent
TM-03

Deposit paid → Move booked

13 nodes, 1 decision.

Watch for

  • Unsigned Stripe calls are rejected.
  • Stripe can send an event twice. The job is booked once.
  • Nudges stop the moment a deposit lands.

Outcome

A paid deposit books the move, and an unpaid estimate never sits forgotten.

Trigger

Stripe checkout.session.completed, plus an hourly sweep.

Exits

  • Booked
  • Nudged
  • Lost after two nudges
TM-04

Move week → Crew dispatched

9 nodes, 2 decisions.

Watch for

  • A missing crew two days out is the one alert that must not be missed.
  • Dates are compared in Central time, not UTC.

Outcome

Every move has a crew, and a client who knows when they arrive.

Trigger

Schedule, every day at 07:00 Central.

Exits

  • Packing guide sent
  • Crew confirmed
  • Ops alerted
  • Arrival window sent
TM-05

Move done → Paid + reviewed

14 nodes, 1 decision.

Watch for

  • A damage mention always goes to a person, never to an auto-reply.
  • The review link is only sent after the invoice is paid.

Outcome

Every finished move is paid, and every reply lands with the right person.

Trigger

The crew sets status to Done on the Crew schedule sheet.

Exits

  • Closed with a review link
  • Claim open
  • Owner follow-up
TM-06

Cancel or reschedule

11 nodes, 2 decisions.

Watch for

  • The refund amount comes from the Code node, never from Claude.
  • A $0 refund skips Stripe but still confirms.

Outcome

Changes are handled the same day, with refunds that follow the policy exactly.

Trigger

The reschedule form posts to /tm/change.

Exits

  • New date confirmed
  • Three dates offered
  • Cancelled and refunded

07

Build order

Five phases, in the order they are built.

Order

Foundation first, then the workflows that take money, then the ones that run on their own.

  1. 1 · Foundation

    • Create the Supabase tables: contacts, jobs, events
    • Add the credentials: TM · Supabase, TM · Stripe (test), TM · Gmail, TM · Telegram, TM · Anthropic
    • Create the Sheets tabs: Quote log, Rate card, Crew schedule, Test matrix, Errors
    • Build TM-H01, TM-H02 and TM-H03, and set TM-H01 as the error workflow

  2. 2 · Intake

    • Build TM-01 and point the site’s quote form at /tm/quote
    • Build TM-02 with the Cal.com webhook and the approval form
    • Run tests t01–t08

  3. 3 · Money

    • Build TM-03 with the Stripe webhook and the hourly sweep
    • Build TM-05 with the invoice and the feedback steps
    • Run tests t09–t12, t16 and t17

  4. 4 · Operations

    • Build TM-04
    • Build TM-06 with the refund policy
    • Run tests t13–t15 and t18–t20

  5. 5 · Test and hand over

    • Switch Stripe to live keys
    • Run the whole matrix again with a real card and a real refund
    • Walk the owner through the handover page
    • Turn every workflow on
08

Handover

What the owner keeps.

Written for the owner

One page, no jargon. It lives next to the workflows.

What it does

  • Answers every quote within 5 minutes, or asks one question.
  • Books surveys and drafts estimates for you to approve.
  • Takes deposits, fills the crew calendar and chases unpaid estimates.
  • Reminds clients and crews before every move.
  • Invoices, asks for feedback and sends damage reports to you.

What breaks it

  • Renaming or moving a column in the Crew schedule sheet.
  • An expired or rotated Stripe key.
  • Changing the Cal.com event type or its questions.
  • Turning a workflow off without turning off its schedule.

Who to call

Justine Acosta, who built it. Errors already reach the ops Telegram group with a link to the run.

Where the logs are

Every failure is a row in the Errors tab and a Telegram message. Every job change is a row in the Supabase events table.
